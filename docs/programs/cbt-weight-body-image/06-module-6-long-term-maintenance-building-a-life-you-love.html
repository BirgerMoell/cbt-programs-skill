Module 6 • Long-Term Maintenance: Building a Life You Love

Overview
  • The final module focuses on maintaining progress and handling setbacks. We acknowledge that change is not linear.
  • We explore 'Lapse vs. Relapse'—understanding that one difficult day or week doesn't mean you've failed.
  • You will create a 'Maintenance Toolkit'—strategies to return to when you feel yourself slipping into old patterns.
  • We practice 'Self-Compassion'—treating yourself with kindness when you struggle, not criticism.
  • The concept of 'Progress, Not Perfection' is reinforced. Celebrating small wins and learning from challenges.
  • We address 'Social Support'—building a network of people who support your journey without enabling diet culture.
  • Finally, we write a 'Body Wisdom Manifesto'—a personal statement of your values and intentions for your relationship with food and body going forward.
Key Takeaways
  • Setbacks are normal and expected; they don't mean failure.
  • Self-compassion is more effective than self-criticism for long-term change.
  • Progress is measured in behaviors and mindset, not just weight.
  • You have the tools to handle any challenge that arises.

The journey continues

This program is not a destination. It's a beginning. You've learned new skills, but mastery takes time. You will have good days and hard days. That's normal.

The goal isn't perfection. The goal is progress. Progress in how you think about food. Progress in how you treat your body. Progress in how you cope with emotions.

You are building a new relationship—with food, with your body, with yourself. Relationships take time. Be patient. Be kind. Trust the process.

When you stumble

You will have days when you eat emotionally. You will have days when you body check obsessively. You will have days when you criticize your body. This doesn't mean you've failed.

A lapse is a temporary return to old patterns. A relapse is giving up entirely. The difference? How you respond. Do you beat yourself up and quit? Or do you pause, learn, and continue?

Self-compassion is your superpower. When you're kind to yourself, you can get back on track faster. When you're cruel, you spiral. Choose kindness.

Your body wisdom manifesto

You've learned a lot. You've changed a lot. Now it's time to write it down. What do you believe about food? About your body? About yourself? What are your intentions going forward?

This manifesto is your anchor. When you feel lost, read it. When you doubt yourself, read it. It reminds you of who you're becoming and what you value.

You are not the same person who started this program. You are wiser, kinder, more aware. Trust that wisdom. Let it guide you.

Homework
  • Review your Maintenance Toolkit weekly. Add new tools as you discover them. Remove tools that don't work for you.
  • Practice Self-Compassion Daily: When you struggle, say 'This is hard. I'm doing my best. I can learn from this and continue.'
  • Share your Body Wisdom Manifesto with someone you trust. Ask them to remind you of it when you forget.
  • Continue your Food & Mood Journal, but reduce frequency to 2-3 times per week. Use it as a check-in tool, not a daily requirement.
  • Celebrate your progress. Make a list of 10 ways you've changed (e.g., 'I eat breakfast regularly,' 'I'm kinder to my body,' 'I have tools for emotional eating'). Read this when you doubt your progress.
