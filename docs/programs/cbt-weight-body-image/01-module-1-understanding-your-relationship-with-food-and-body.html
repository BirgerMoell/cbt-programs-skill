CBT Program

Module 1 • Understanding Your Relationship with Food and Body

Overview
  • We begin by exploring your personal history with food, weight, and body image. Understanding where your patterns come from is the first step to changing them.
  • We introduce the concept of 'Food as Medicine vs. Food as Coping.' You will learn to distinguish between physical hunger and emotional hunger.
  • The 'Diet-Binge Cycle' is explained. We explore how restriction leads to overeating, creating a pattern that feels impossible to break.
  • We challenge the myth that weight loss is simply about 'willpower.' You will learn about the complex biological, psychological, and social factors that influence eating behaviors.
  • You will start a 'Food & Mood Journal.' This tracks not just what you eat, but when, why, and how you feel—revealing the emotional patterns behind eating.
  • We introduce 'Body Image Distortion'—how your perception of your body may not match reality, and how this perception is shaped by internalized messages.
  • Finally, we set a new goal: The aim isn't just weight loss, but building a life where food and your body are sources of joy, not anxiety.
Key Takeaways
  • Eating behaviors are complex and often serve emotional needs beyond nutrition.
  • Restriction almost always leads to rebound eating—the body fights back.
  • Your relationship with food mirrors your relationship with yourself.
  • Body image is a mental construct, not a mirror reflection.

The war on your body

If you are here, you have likely been at war with your body for a long time. You have tried diets, exercise plans, and willpower. Maybe you lost weight, but it came back. Maybe you never lost it. Either way, you are exhausted.

The diet industry profits from your pain. It tells you that if you just try harder, buy the right program, or follow the right rules, you will finally be happy. But this is a lie. The problem isn't you—it's the approach.

We are going to stop fighting. Instead, we are going to listen. Your body has wisdom. It knows when it's hungry, when it's full, and when it needs movement. We have just been taught to ignore these signals.

Food is not the enemy

Food is not good or bad. It is just food. When we label foods as 'forbidden' or 'sinful,' we create a psychological magnet. The more we restrict, the more we crave.

Think of a child told not to touch something. What do they want to do? Touch it. Your brain works the same way. When you tell yourself 'I can't have that,' you create a mental obsession.

We are going to remove the labels. We are going to learn to eat with awareness, not rules. We are going to trust that when you allow all foods, you naturally gravitate toward what nourishes you.

Exercise 2

The Hunger Audit: For one day, before every eating occasion, rate your physical hunger (1-10). Also note your emotional state. Are you eating from hunger or emotion?

The body image mirror

When you look in the mirror, what do you see? Do you see your whole self, or just the parts you want to change? Body image is not about how you look—it's about how you see.

We have been taught to see our bodies as projects to fix. But your body is not a project. It is your home. It carries you through life. It deserves respect, not constant criticism.

We are going to practice seeing your body with fresh eyes. We are going to notice what it can do, not just how it looks. We are going to shift from judgment to curiosity.

Exercise 3

The Body Image Check-In: Stand in front of a mirror. List 5 things your body does for you (e.g., 'allows me to hug my children'). Then list 5 things you see that you appreciate (e.g., 'my eyes show kindness').

Homework
  • Keep a Food & Mood Journal for 7 days. Record: time, food, hunger level (1-10), emotions before/after, and context (alone, stressed, celebrating).
  • Practice 'Neutral Body Language': For one week, when you catch yourself criticizing your body, say 'This is my body. It is neither good nor bad. It just is.'
  • Identify one 'forbidden food' and allow yourself to have it mindfully, without guilt. Notice what happens to the craving when permission is granted.
