What is Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT)?

CBT helps you see the link between Thoughts (what you tell yourself), Feelings (what you experience), and Behaviours (what you do).

For ADHD, CBT often means practical strategies for planning, starting tasks, staying focused, and regulating emotions—so your actions line up with your values.

Understanding your ADHD experience

ADHD shows up differently for everyone—commonly as inattention, hyperactivity, and impulsivity. These patterns are differences in brain function, not character flaws. Identifying your patterns is the first step toward effective strategies.

Your brain's two modes: the Dreamer and the Doer

Think of the Default Mode Network (DMN) as the Dreamer—it plans, imagines, and reflects. The Task Positive Network (TPN) is the Doer—it executes with focused attention.

With ADHD, the bridge between Dreamer and Doer can be unreliable. You can strengthen it through small, precise, immediate commitments.

  • Define 1–3 SMART process goals for the next 7 days (e.g., "Do a 5‑minute planning check each evening").
Homework
  • Reflect on inattention/hyperactivity/impulsivity this week—capture two specific situations without judgment.
  • List strengths and supports you bring (creativity, humor, persistence, allies).
  • Write a one‑sentence self‑compassion pledge to revisit on hard days.
  • Practice one micro‑intention daily. Note: planned time, action, did it (Y/N).
