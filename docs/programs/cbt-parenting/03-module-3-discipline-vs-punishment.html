Module 3 • Discipline vs. Punishment

Overview
  • We clarify the difference: Punishment is about making a child pay for a mistake (fear-based). Discipline is about teaching a child how to do better (growth-based).
  • We introduce 'Natural and Logical Consequences' instead of arbitrary punishments.
  • The concept of 'Connection before Correction.' You must re-establish the relationship bridge before you can drive the teaching truck across it.
  • Moving away from shame and threats.
Key Takeaways
  • Punishment suppresses behavior; discipline changes character.
  • A child who feels bad behaves bad. A child who feels connected behaves better.
  • Consequences should be related, reasonable, and respectful.

Building the bridge

Imagine trying to drive a truck across a bridge that has collapsed. It won't work. When a child is acting out, the bridge of connection is broken. If you try to drive the 'Correction Truck' (advice, scolding) across it, you will crash.

First, rebuild the bridge. Get on their level. Empathize with their feeling ('You really wanted that cookie'). Once the bridge is strong, *then* you can drive the truck ('But we don't hit when we are mad').

Exercise 1

The Empathy Guess: Before correcting, guess the child's feeling out loud. 'You seem really frustrated that playtime is over.'

Homework

  • Design one logical consequence for a recurring behavior.
