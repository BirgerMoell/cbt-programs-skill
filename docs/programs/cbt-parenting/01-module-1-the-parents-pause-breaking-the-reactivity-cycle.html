Module 1 • The Parent’s Pause: Breaking the Reactivity Cycle

Overview
  • We begin by normalizing parental anger and overwhelm. You are not a 'bad parent' for losing your cool; you are a dysregulated human.
  • We introduce the biology of the 'Flipped Lid' (Dan Siegel’s model). When you are shouting, your prefrontal cortex is offline. You cannot parent effectively from this state.
  • The core skill of 'The Pause' is introduced. Learning to stop the action between the trigger (spilled milk) and the reaction (yelling).
  • We map your 'Shark Music.' Identifying the specific physiological cues that signal you are about to explode.
Key Takeaways
  • A dysregulated parent cannot regulate a dysregulated child.
  • The 'Pause' is the most powerful tool in your parenting toolkit.
  • Anger is a signal that a boundary has been crossed or a resource is depleted.

The shark music

Imagine a movie scene where a character is swimming peacefully. Suddenly, the ominous 'shark music' starts playing. You know something bad is coming.

In parenting, you have shark music too. Maybe it’s a tight chest, a clenched jaw, or a sudden thought like 'They never listen!' When you hear the music, the shark (the explosion) is coming.

Our goal is not to stop the music from playing, but to hear it early enough to get out of the water. When you hear it, you stop. You breathe. You step away. You do not parent until the music stops.
