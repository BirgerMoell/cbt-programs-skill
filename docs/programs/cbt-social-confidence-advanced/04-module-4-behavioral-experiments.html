CBT Program

Module 4 • Behavioral Experiments

Overview
  • ### Introduction: From Exposure to Experiments Old-school therapy says "face your fears." We say "test your hypotheses." We are going to treat social situations as a laboratory. Instead of just gritting your teeth and enduring a party, you will enter it with a specific question: "If I pause while speaking, will people really walk away?" We call these **Behavioral Experiments**.
  • ### Section 2: Building the Fear Ladder We don't start with the hardest thing (like giving a speech). We build a hierarchy. We list situations from 0 (easy) to 100 (terrifying). We start in the middle—the "Stretch Zone." This ensures you have success early on and builds your confidence systematically.
  • ### Section 3: Inhibitory Learning The goal of an experiment is not for you to feel calm; it is for you to learn that the catastrophe didn't happen. Even if you felt anxious, did you survive? Did people attack you? This creates **Inhibitory Learning**—your brain creates a new "safe" memory that overrides the old "danger" memory.
Key Takeaways
  • We avoid situations to avoid feelings, but avoidance makes the fear stronger.
  • Action precedes confidence. You must do the thing *before* you feel ready.
  • Success is defined by *doing* the experiment, not by a lack of anxiety.

No narrative sections.

Exercises
  • **Designing the Fear Ladder** Brainstorm 10 social scenarios. Rate them 1-100 on a SUDS scale (Subjective Units of Distress). Order them from easiest to hardest.
  • **Experiment Planning Sheet** Select an item from the ladder (rated ~40/100). 1. **Prediction:** "If I ask for the time, they will ignore me." 2. **Experiment:** Ask a stranger for the time. 3. **Result:** "They told me the time." 4. **Learning:** "My prediction was wrong."
  • **Shame Attacking (Advanced)** Deliberately do something minorly silly (e.g., ask for a discount on a coffee that isn't on sale) to realize that even if you are "awkward," the world doesn't end.
Homework
  • Conduct two small behavioral experiments from your ladder this week.
  • Immediately after the experiment, complete the 'Learning' section of your sheet. This is crucial for rewiring the brain.
  • Reward yourself! Reinforce your bravery with something you enjoy immediately after the exposure.
