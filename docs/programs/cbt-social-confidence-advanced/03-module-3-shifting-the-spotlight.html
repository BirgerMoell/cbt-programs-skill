Module 3 • Shifting the Spotlight

Overview
  • ### Introduction: The Spotlight Effect When we are anxious, our attention turns inward. We monitor our heart rate, our words, and our posture. This is self-focused attention. The problem is, when you are looking at yourself, you cannot look at the world. You miss the smiles, the nods, and the cues of safety. You also hallucinate that everyone is noticing your anxiety as much as you are—this is the **Spotlight Effect**.
  • ### Section 2: External Focus Training The antidote to self-consciousness is not "trying to relax," it is curiosity. We practice **External Focus**—deliberately shifting your attention to the sights, sounds, and people around you. When you are truly listening to someone else's voice or noticing the color of the walls, your brain has less processing power to dedicate to worrying about yourself.
  • ### Section 3: Dropping the Shield Now that we understand Safety Behaviors (from Module 1), it is time to experiment with dropping them. What happens if you *don't* check your phone? What happens if you let a silence hang for 5 seconds? We will prepare to enter social situations "unarmed," trusting that you can handle whatever happens.
Key Takeaways
  • You cannot be fully present and fully self-conscious at the same time.
  • People notice far less about your appearance/anxiety than you think (The Spotlight Effect).
  • Shifting focus outward reduces the feedback loop of physical symptoms.

Exercises
  • **Attention Training Technique (ATT)** A daily 5-minute practice: Close your eyes and focus on 3 sounds outside the room, then 3 sounds inside the room, then your breath. Switch rapidly. This strengthens your ability to *command* your attention.
  • **The Curiosity Drill** In your next conversation, make it your goal to find out three specific things about the other person. Focus entirely on *their* answers, not your next question.
  • **The "Video Camera" Perspective** Visualize your last anxious interaction. First, view it from your own eyes (internal). Then, reimagine it from a security camera in the corner (external). Notice how the "camera" likely saw a normal person, not a nervous wreck.
Homework
  • Take a 'Sensory Walk': Walk for 15 minutes, focusing strictly on colors and textures. If your mind goes to worry, gently return it to a specific color.
  • Engage in one conversation where you deliberately drop a safety behavior (e.g., keep your hands visible on the table instead of hiding them).
  • Rate your self-focus percentage (0-100%) after interactions. Aim to lower the number.
