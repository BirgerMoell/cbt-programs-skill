Module 1 • The Filter Problem

Overview
  • We begin by validating the distress. Tinnitus is not 'just a noise'; it is a phantom perception that can trigger intense fight-or-flight reactions.
  • We explain the 'Jastreboff Neurophysiological Model.' The problem isn't the ear; it's the limbic system (emotion center) treating the sound as a threat.
  • The concept of 'Habituation' is introduced using the 'Refrigerator Hum' analogy. Your brain ignores steady sounds unless it thinks they are dangerous.
  • We map the 'Vicious Cycle': Tinnitus → Fear/Focus → Increased Volume Perception → More Fear.
  • We introduce 'Sound Enrichment.' Using background noise not to mask (drown out) the tinnitus, but to reduce the contrast, making it easier to ignore.
Key Takeaways
  • Tinnitus volume is subjective and fluctuates based on your emotional state.
  • You cannot habituate to a sound you are afraid of.
  • The goal is not silence, but indifference.

The tiger in the room

If you heard a tiger growling in the corner of your room, you would not be able to ignore it. Your brain would lock onto that sound. You wouldn't sleep. You wouldn't read. You would just listen.

Right now, your brain thinks your tinnitus is a tiger. It is monitoring the sound 24/7 to keep you 'safe.' But tinnitus is not a tiger. It is a paper tiger. It cannot hurt you.

Our job is to teach your brain that this sound is as boring and safe as the hum of a refrigerator. Once the brain realizes it is safe, it will naturally stop listening.
