Module 2 • Cognitive Defusion: Changing the Story

Overview
  • We tackle the catastrophic thoughts associated with tinnitus: 'It will never stop,' 'I will go crazy,' 'I have lost my silence.'
  • We use Cognitive Defusion. Seeing thoughts as just thoughts, not facts.
  • We challenge the 'Silence Myth.' True silence is rare and unnatural. We redefine peace not as the absence of sound, but the absence of distress.
  • We address the grief of hearing loss (if present) and the anger at the cause (e.g., loud concerts, medication).
Key Takeaways
  • Thoughts like 'I can't cope' amplify the distress more than the sound itself.
  • Fighting the sound creates tension, which makes the sound louder.
  • Acceptance is not giving up; it is dropping the rope in a tug-of-war.

Dropping the rope

Imagine you are in a tug-of-war with a monster. Between you is a pit. You pull and pull, trying to drag the monster into the pit. You are exhausted. Your hands bleed.

The monster is tinnitus. You can't drag it into the pit. But you can drop the rope. The monster is still there, on the other side of the pit, making noise. But you are free. You can use your hands for something else now.
