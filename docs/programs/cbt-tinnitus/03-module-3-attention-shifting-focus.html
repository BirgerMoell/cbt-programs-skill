Module 3 • Attention Shifting & Focus

Overview
  • Tinnitus dominates attention. This module trains the brain to shift focus at will.
  • We introduce 'Active Listening' to other sounds. Training the auditory filter to pick up birds, traffic, or music *over* the tinnitus.
  • We practice 'The Spotlight.' Moving your mental spotlight from the ear to the foot, the hand, the visual field.
  • We discuss 'Monitoring.' The habit of checking 'Is it still there?' We learn to stop checking.
Key Takeaways
  • Where attention goes, neural firing flows.
  • Checking the tinnitus resets the habituation process.
  • You can enjoy a movie even if the person behind you is eating popcorn loudly. Tinnitus is just popcorn.

The party effect

Imagine being at a loud party. You can focus on one conversation and tune out the rest. Your brain does this automatically. It filters out the 'noise.'

Currently, your brain has the tinnitus on the main stage. We are going to move it to the background noise of the party. You will still hear it if you listen for it, but you will be too busy with the main conversation (your life) to care.

Exercise 1

Sound Scavenger Hunt: Go for a walk. actively identify 5 distinct sounds (wind, cars, leaves). Focus intently on each one for 1 minute.

Exercises
  • Sound Scavenger Hunt: Go for a walk. actively identify 5 distinct sounds (wind, cars, leaves). Focus intently on each one for 1 minute.
  • The Shift Drill: Focus on the tinnitus for 10 seconds. Then shift focus to the sensation of your feet on the floor for 10 seconds. Repeat.
Homework
  • Practice the Sound Scavenger Hunt daily.
  • When you notice tinnitus, immediately engage in a task that requires concentration (e.g., a puzzle, a conversation).
