Module 2 • Overcoming Barriers: Time, Energy, and Access

Overview
  • This module provides practical strategies for the most common barriers to exercise: lack of time, low energy, and limited access to facilities or equipment.
  • We introduce 'Micro-Movements'—short bursts of activity that fit into busy schedules. You'll learn that 10 minutes counts, and consistency matters more than duration.
  • You will create a 'Movement Menu'—a list of activities you can do with different time constraints (5 min, 15 min, 30 min, 60 min).
  • We explore 'Energy Management'—matching activity intensity to your current energy level. Some days are for vigorous movement; others are for gentle stretching.
  • The concept of 'Home-Based Movement' is introduced. You don't need a gym or equipment to be active. Your body weight and creativity are enough.
  • We address 'Scheduling Strategies'—how to fit movement into your existing routine without it feeling like another task.
  • Finally, we practice 'Barrier Problem-Solving'—a systematic approach to identifying and overcoming obstacles as they arise.
Key Takeaways
  • Time constraints are real, but movement doesn't require large blocks of time.
  • Low energy days still allow for gentle movement—often movement increases energy.
  • You can be active anywhere, with or without equipment.
  • Problem-solving barriers is a skill that improves with practice.

The time myth

'I don't have time' is the most common barrier to exercise. But here's the truth: You have the same 24 hours as everyone else. The question isn't whether you have time—it's whether movement is a priority.

We're not asking you to find an hour a day. We're asking you to find 10 minutes. Or 5. Or even 2. Micro-movements add up. A 5-minute walk here, a 10-minute stretch there. Before you know it, you've moved for 30 minutes.

We're also going to look at where your time actually goes. How much time do you spend scrolling? Watching TV? Those aren't bad—but if movement matters, we can find small pockets.

Exercise 1

Create Your Movement Menu: List activities for different time slots: 5 min (e.g., stretch, walk around block), 15 min (e.g., brisk walk, bodyweight circuit), 30 min (e.g., longer walk, yoga video), 60 min (e.g., hike, full workout).

Energy follows movement

It's counterintuitive, but movement often creates energy. When you're tired, the last thing you want to do is move. But a gentle walk or stretch can wake you up.

We're going to match movement to energy. On high-energy days, do more. On low-energy days, do less—but do something. Even 5 minutes of movement is better than zero.

We're also going to look at when you have energy. Are you a morning person? Evening person? Schedule movement when your energy is naturally higher.

Exercise 2

The Time Audit: Track your time for 3 days. Where does it go? Identify 3 'movement opportunities'—times when you could fit in 5-10 minutes of activity.

Your body is your gym

You don't need a gym membership. You don't need fancy equipment. You have everything you need: your body, gravity, and creativity.

Bodyweight exercises (squats, push-ups, planks) are free and effective. Walking is free. Dancing is free. Stretching is free. The barrier isn't access—it's often mindset.

We're going to build a movement practice that works with what you have, where you are, right now.

Exercise 3

The Energy-Movement Match: For one week, rate your energy (1-10) before movement. Choose activity intensity to match. Notice how movement affects your energy afterward.

Homework
  • Use your Movement Menu this week. When you have 5 minutes, do a 5-minute activity. When you have 15, do a 15-minute activity. Notice how small movements add up.
  • Schedule 3 movement sessions this week. Put them in your calendar like appointments. Start with just 10 minutes each.
  • Try one home-based movement activity you've never done before (e.g., YouTube yoga, bodyweight circuit, dance video).
