Module 1 • Understanding Your Relationship with Exercise

Overview
  • We begin by exploring your personal history with physical activity. What activities have you enjoyed? What barriers have stopped you? Understanding your past is key to building your future.
  • We identify your 'Exercise Beliefs'—the thoughts and assumptions that shape your relationship with movement. Many people hold limiting beliefs like 'I'm not athletic' or 'Exercise is punishment.'
  • You will learn to distinguish between 'Exercise' (structured, often dreaded) and 'Movement' (any physical activity that feels good). This reframe opens up possibilities.
  • We introduce the concept of 'Readiness for Change.' Where are you on the spectrum from 'not thinking about it' to 'actively maintaining'? This determines your starting point.
  • You will complete a 'Barrier Assessment'—identifying the specific obstacles that prevent you from being active (time, energy, fear, motivation, access, etc.).
  • We explore 'Activity Preferences'—what types of movement do you actually enjoy? Not what you think you should do, but what feels good in your body.
  • Finally, we set realistic, process-based goals. Instead of 'lose 20 pounds,' we focus on 'move 3 times this week' or 'feel stronger.'
Key Takeaways
  • Your past relationship with exercise doesn't define your future.
  • Movement doesn't have to be intense or structured to be effective.
  • Identifying barriers is the first step to overcoming them.
  • Process goals (actions) are more sustainable than outcome goals (results).

The exercise paradox

You know exercise is good for you. You've tried to start countless times. You've joined gyms, bought equipment, downloaded apps. But it never sticks. Why?

Often, the problem isn't laziness—it's approach. We've been taught that exercise must be hard, sweaty, and painful to count. We've been taught to push through discomfort, ignore our bodies, and measure success by calories burned or miles run.

But sustainable movement is different. It's about listening to your body, finding activities you enjoy, and building gradually. It's about consistency over intensity. It's about movement as self-care, not punishment.

From exercise to movement

The word 'exercise' carries baggage. It implies structure, effort, and obligation. But movement is broader. Walking your dog is movement. Dancing in your kitchen is movement. Gardening is movement. Taking the stairs is movement.

When you shift from 'I need to exercise' to 'I get to move my body,' everything changes. Movement becomes a privilege, not a chore. You start noticing opportunities instead of obstacles.

We're going to expand your definition of what counts. Every step, every stretch, every moment of physical activity matters. We're going to celebrate small wins and build from there.

Your barriers are real

Time is limited. Energy is finite. Motivation fluctuates. These aren't excuses—they're realities. But they're not insurmountable.

When you identify your specific barriers, you can problem-solve. If time is the issue, we find 10-minute activities. If energy is low, we do gentle movement. If motivation is missing, we build systems that don't require it.

Your barriers are information, not roadblocks. They tell us what you need. Let's listen.

Homework
  • Complete your Barrier Assessment. For each barrier, brainstorm 2-3 potential solutions. Don't worry if they're perfect—just get ideas flowing.
  • Try one new type of movement this week. It doesn't have to be structured. Just move your body in a way that feels good.
  • Set 3 process-based goals for the next week (e.g., 'walk 10 minutes 3 times,' 'stretch every morning,' 'dance to one song daily').
