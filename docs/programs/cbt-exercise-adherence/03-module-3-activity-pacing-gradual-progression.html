Module 3 • Activity Pacing & Gradual Progression

Overview
  • This module teaches the critical skill of 'Activity Pacing'—building activity gradually to prevent injury, burnout, and the boom-bust cycle.
  • We introduce the '10% Rule'—increasing activity by no more than 10% per week to allow your body to adapt safely.
  • You will learn to identify your 'Baseline'—the amount of activity you can do consistently without pain or excessive fatigue.
  • The concept of 'Progressive Overload' is explained in accessible terms. Your body adapts to what you ask of it, but gradually.
  • We address the 'Weekend Warrior' trap—doing too much on good days and crashing afterward. We'll smooth out the peaks and valleys.
  • You will create a 'Pacing Plan'—a structured approach to gradually increasing your activity over time.
  • Finally, we practice 'Listening to Your Body'—recognizing the difference between good discomfort (muscle fatigue) and bad pain (injury signals).
Key Takeaways
  • Gradual progression prevents injury and builds sustainable habits.
  • Consistency beats intensity every time.
  • Your baseline is your foundation—build from there, not from where you 'should' be.
  • Pain is information; learn to read it correctly.

The tortoise wins

You're excited. You want to see results fast. So you go all in: an hour at the gym, every day, pushing hard. By week two, you're injured or exhausted. You quit. Sound familiar?

This is the boom-bust cycle. It feels productive, but it's actually counterproductive. Your body needs time to adapt. Your nervous system needs time to adjust. Your habits need time to form.

The tortoise approach—slow, steady, consistent—wins the race. Small increases, week by week. Building a foundation that lasts.

Exercise 1

Establish Your Baseline: For one week, do the same activity (e.g., walk 10 min) every day. Note: Can you do it consistently? Any pain or excessive fatigue? This is your baseline.

Finding your baseline

Your baseline is where you are right now, not where you want to be. If you can walk 10 minutes without pain, that's your baseline. If you can do 5 push-ups, that's your baseline.

We're going to test your baseline honestly. No comparing to others. No 'shoulds.' Just: What can you do consistently, right now, without negative consequences?

Once you know your baseline, you can build. 10 minutes becomes 11. 5 push-ups becomes 6. Slowly, safely, sustainably.

Exercise 2

Create Your Pacing Plan: Starting from your baseline, plan 4 weeks of gradual increases (10% per week). Write it down. Stick to it, even if you feel you could do more.

The 10% rule

The 10% rule is simple: Don't increase activity by more than 10% per week. If you walk 20 minutes this week, next week you can walk 22 minutes. If you do 10 push-ups, next week you can do 11.

This feels slow. But slow is sustainable. Slow prevents injury. Slow builds confidence. Slow creates habits that stick.

We're playing the long game. Not 'how much can I do today?' but 'how much can I do consistently for months?'

Homework
  • Follow your Pacing Plan for 4 weeks. Resist the urge to do more on 'good days.' Consistency is the goal.
  • Practice the 10% Rule with one activity. Track your progress. Notice how gradual increases feel sustainable.
  • If you experience pain (not just discomfort), stop. Rest. Assess. Don't push through—that's how injuries happen.
