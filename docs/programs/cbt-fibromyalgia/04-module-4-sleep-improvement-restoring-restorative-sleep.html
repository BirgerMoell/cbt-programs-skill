Module 4 • Sleep Improvement: Restoring Restorative Sleep

Overview
  • This module addresses the critical role of sleep in fibromyalgia management, as poor sleep directly worsens pain and fatigue.
  • We explore 'Why Sleep Matters'—how sleep disruption creates a vicious cycle with pain and fatigue.
  • You will learn 'Sleep Hygiene Principles'—creating conditions that promote better sleep (routine, environment, behaviors).
  • The concept of 'Sleep Restriction' is introduced—limiting time in bed to improve sleep efficiency (adapted for fibromyalgia).

  • You will practice 'Relaxation for Sleep'—techniques to calm the nervous system before bed.
  • Finally, we create a 'Sleep Improvement Plan'—a personalized approach to better sleep.
Key Takeaways
  • Sleep quality directly impacts pain and fatigue in fibromyalgia.
  • Sleep hygiene creates conditions for better sleep.
  • Pain management at night improves sleep; better sleep reduces pain.
  • Improving sleep is a gradual process requiring consistency.

The sleep-pain cycle

Poor sleep worsens pain. Pain disrupts sleep. It's a cycle that feels impossible to break. But it's not.

When you don't sleep well, your pain threshold drops. Your nervous system becomes more sensitive. Fatigue increases. Everything feels harder.

But when you improve sleep, even slightly, you can break the cycle. Better sleep reduces pain sensitivity. It reduces fatigue. It improves your ability to cope.

Creating sleep conditions

You can't force sleep, but you can create conditions that invite it. A consistent routine. A comfortable environment. Relaxation before bed. Managing pain.

Sleep hygiene isn't about rigid rules. It's about creating a sleep-friendly lifestyle. One that signals to your body: 'It's time to rest.'

We're going to build these conditions. We're going to make sleep a priority. Because in fibromyalgia, sleep is medicine.

Exercise 2

The Sleep Environment Audit: Evaluate your bedroom: temperature, light, noise, comfort. What could be improved?

Sleep restriction for fibromyalgia

Sleep restriction—limiting time in bed to match actual sleep time—can improve sleep efficiency. But it needs to be adapted for fibromyalgia, where rest is also important.

We're going to use a gentle approach. Not strict restriction, but optimization. Finding the right amount of time in bed. Creating quality over quantity.

This might mean going to bed later or getting up earlier. It might feel counterintuitive. But better sleep efficiency often means better sleep quality, which means less pain and fatigue.

Homework
  • Implement sleep hygiene improvements: consistent bedtime/wake time, cool/dark/quiet bedroom, no screens 1 hour before bed.
  • Practice your Pre-Sleep Routine every night for 2 weeks. Notice impact on sleep quality.
  • If pain disrupts sleep, try pain management techniques before bed (gentle stretching, heat, relaxation, medication if prescribed).
