Module 5 • Cognitive Restructuring & Values-Based Living

Overview
  • This module addresses the psychological aspects of living with fibromyalgia, including challenging unhelpful thoughts and building a meaningful life despite symptoms.
  • We explore 'Pain Catastrophizing'—exaggerated negative thoughts about pain that worsen the experience.
  • You will learn 'Cognitive Restructuring'—challenging unhelpful thoughts and replacing them with balanced perspectives.
  • The concept of 'Acceptance vs. Resignation' is introduced—accepting the reality of fibromyalgia without giving up on living well.
  • We address 'Values-Based Living'—identifying what matters to you and finding ways to engage with those values despite limitations.
  • You will practice 'Activity Engagement Despite Pain'—participating in meaningful activities even when symptoms are present.
  • Finally, we create a 'Life Values Plan'—identifying your values and planning activities that align with them.
Key Takeaways
  • Thoughts about pain influence pain experience.
  • Acceptance doesn't mean giving up—it means working with reality.
  • Living according to your values improves quality of life despite symptoms.
  • You can have a meaningful life with fibromyalgia.

The thoughts that amplify

Your thoughts about pain matter. 'This will never end' amplifies suffering. 'I can't do anything' creates helplessness. 'My life is over' creates despair.

These thoughts aren't facts. They're interpretations. And interpretations can be changed.

We're going to challenge these thoughts. We're going to find balanced perspectives. We're going to reduce the power of catastrophic thinking.

Exercise 1

The Catastrophic Thought Challenge: When pain or fatigue is high, write down your thoughts. Challenge each: 'Is this helpful? Is it true? What's a more balanced perspective?'

Acceptance, not resignation

Acceptance means acknowledging reality: 'I have fibromyalgia. I have pain and fatigue. This is my current situation.'

Resignation means giving up: 'There's nothing I can do. My life is over. I might as well stop trying.'

Acceptance is powerful. It allows you to work with reality instead of fighting it. It opens possibilities. Resignation closes them.

We're going to practice acceptance. We're going to acknowledge limitations while finding ways to live well within them.

Exercise 2

The Values Identification: List 10 things that matter to you (relationships, creativity, nature, learning, etc.). Rank your top 5 values.

Living your values

Fibromyalgia might limit what you can do, but it doesn't have to limit who you are. You can still live according to your values.

What matters to you? Connection? Creativity? Contribution? Learning? These values can guide your choices, even with limitations.

We're going to identify your values. We're going to find ways to engage with them. We're going to build a meaningful life, adapted but not abandoned.

Exercise 3

The Values-Activity Connection: For each of your top 5 values, list 3 activities that align with it and are still possible with fibromyalgia (even if adapted).

Homework
  • Challenge 3 catastrophic thoughts this week. Write them down. Find balanced alternatives. Notice how this affects your experience.
  • Engage in one values-based activity this week, even if symptoms are present. Adapt as needed. Notice how meaning affects your experience.
  • Practice acceptance statements: 'I have fibromyalgia, and I can still live a meaningful life.' 'Pain is present, and I can still engage in activities I value.'
