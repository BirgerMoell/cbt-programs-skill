Module 2 • Activity Pacing & Energy Conservation

Overview
  • This module addresses one of the most critical skills for managing fibromyalgia: Activity Pacing—balancing activity and rest to prevent the boom-bust cycle.
  • We explore 'The Boom-Bust Cycle'—doing too much on good days and crashing afterward, which perpetuates symptoms.
  • You will learn to identify your 'Activity Baselines'—the amount of activity you can do consistently without flaring symptoms.
  • The concept of 'Time-Contingent vs. Pain-Contingent Activity' is introduced. Stopping based on time, not pain, prevents overdoing.
  • We address 'Energy Conservation'—strategies for doing daily tasks with less energy expenditure.
  • You will create a 'Pacing Plan'—a structured approach to gradually increasing activity while respecting your body's limits.
  • Finally, we practice 'The 10% Rule'—increasing activity by no more than 10% per week to allow adaptation.
Key Takeaways
  • Pacing prevents the boom-bust cycle and reduces symptom flares.
  • Consistency beats intensity—doing a little regularly is better than doing a lot occasionally.
  • Your baseline is your foundation; build from there, not from where you 'should' be.
  • Energy conservation techniques help you do more with less fatigue.

The weekend warrior trap

You wake up feeling relatively okay. 'Finally!' you think. 'I can catch up.' You clean the house, run errands, maybe exercise. You push through the fatigue. By evening, you're in agony. The next few days, you can barely move.

This is the boom-bust cycle. It feels productive, but it's actually counterproductive. It teaches your nervous system that activity equals danger. It perpetuates the pain-fatigue cycle.

We need to break this pattern. We need to smooth out the peaks and valleys. We need to do a moderate amount consistently, even on good days—especially on good days.

Exercise 1

Establish Your Baselines: For one week, do the same activities daily (e.g., walk 10 min, light housework 15 min). Note: Can you do them consistently? Any flares? This is your baseline.

Finding your baseline

Your baseline is where you are right now, not where you want to be. If you can walk 10 minutes without flaring, that's your baseline. If you can do light housework for 15 minutes, that's your baseline.

We're going to test your baseline honestly. No comparing to others. No 'shoulds.' Just: What can you do consistently, right now, without negative consequences?

Once you know your baseline, you can build. Slowly. Safely. Respecting your body's signals. 10 minutes becomes 11. 15 minutes becomes 16. Gradual progress.

Exercise 2

The Boom-Bust Analysis: Reflect on past weeks. Identify boom days (overdoing) and bust days (crashing). What patterns do you notice? What triggered the booms?

The pacing principle

Pacing means stopping before you're exhausted. It means doing less than you think you can. It means respecting your limits, even when you want to push.

This is frustrating. You want to do more. But pacing works. It reduces flares. It builds tolerance. It breaks the boom-bust cycle.

Think of it like training for a marathon. You don't start by running 26 miles. You start with a mile. You build gradually. Your body adapts. Same principle applies to fibromyalgia.

Exercise 3

Create Your Pacing Plan: Starting from your baseline, plan 4 weeks of gradual increases (10% per week). Include rest breaks. Write it down. Commit to following it.

Homework
  • Follow your Pacing Plan for 4 weeks. Resist the urge to do more on 'good days.' Consistency is the goal.
  • Practice time-contingent pacing: Set a timer. Stop when it goes off, even if you feel you could continue.
  • Use energy conservation techniques: Sit while doing tasks. Break tasks into smaller chunks. Use assistive devices if helpful.
