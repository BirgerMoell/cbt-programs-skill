Key Takeaways
  • Fibromyalgia is a real condition involving central nervous system sensitization.
  • Your symptoms are valid and not 'all in your head'—though your head (thoughts) can influence them.
  • Pain and fatigue are interconnected; managing one helps the other.
  • You are not alone, and there are effective management strategies.

Your experience is real

If you have fibromyalgia, you know the frustration. Widespread pain that moves around. Overwhelming fatigue that doesn't improve with rest. Sleep that doesn't refresh. Brain fog that makes thinking difficult.

You may have been told it's 'all in your head' or that you're 'just stressed.' But fibromyalgia is real. It's a condition where your central nervous system becomes hypersensitive, amplifying pain signals and creating a cascade of symptoms.

Your experience is valid. Your pain is real. Your fatigue is real. And there are ways to manage these symptoms and live well despite them.

The amplified alarm system

Think of your nervous system as an alarm system. In fibromyalgia, the alarm is set too sensitive. Normal sensations—a light touch, a gentle stretch, even temperature changes—can trigger pain signals.

This isn't because your tissues are damaged. It's because your nervous system is amplifying signals. The volume knob is turned up too high.

The good news? You can learn to turn down the volume. Through pacing, stress management, sleep improvement, and cognitive strategies, you can reduce the amplification. You can manage the symptoms.

The symptom web

Fibromyalgia symptoms are interconnected. Pain disrupts sleep. Poor sleep increases pain and fatigue. Fatigue makes activity harder. Inactivity increases stiffness and pain. It's a web.

But this also means that improving one symptom can help others. Better sleep can reduce pain. Pacing activity can reduce fatigue. Stress management can reduce symptom flares.

We're going to work on all of these. We're going to break the cycles. We're going to build a comprehensive management approach.
