Module 7 • Sustainable Balance: Lifestyle as Medicine

Overview
  • Emotional regulation isn't just about what you do in the moment of crisis; it's about the baseline you establish every day.
  • This module focuses on the 'Emotional Bank Account.' Sleep, nutrition, movement, and connection are the pillars that widen your window of tolerance.
  • We create a long-term protocol for staying balanced.
Key Takeaways
  • The HALT trigger: Never make big decisions when Hungry, Angry, Lonely, or Tired.
  • Sleep is emotional first aid: REM sleep processes emotional memories.
  • Gut-Brain Axis: 95% of serotonin is produced in the gut; food affects mood.
  • Routine is safety: predictability calms the nervous system.

The Vulnerability Factors

It is infinitely harder to regulate your emotions if you are sleep-deprived, hungover, or sedentary.

Treating these biological factors is often the first step in emotional health. If you are irritable, check your biology first.

Exercise completes the stress cycle. Animals shake off adrenaline after a chase. We sit in chairs. You must move to process stress hormones.

Exercise 1

The HALT Check: Before any difficult conversation or task, check: Am I Hungry? Angry? Lonely? Tired? Fix the biology first.

The Emotional Bank Account

Every positive action (good sleep, connection, fun) is a deposit. Every stressor is a withdrawal.

If you run your account near zero, a small fee (minor annoyance) causes an overdraft (meltdown).

Keep your balance high so you can afford the 'life tax' of daily stress.

Creating a Regulation Protocol

You need a plan before the storm hits. Your protocol should include:

Daily maintenance (meditation, walk).

Yellow Alert tools (box breathing, step back).

Red Alert tools (ice dive, removal from situation).
