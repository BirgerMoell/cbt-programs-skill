Module 6 • Self-Compassion: The Antidote to Shame

Overview
  • Many people try to regulate emotions by beating themselves up. 'Stop crying! Be stronger!' This actually increases stress hormones.
  • Self-compassion is not weak; it is a physiological regulator. It activates the care-giving system (oxytocin) to soothe the threat system (cortisol).
  • This module teaches you to treat yourself with the same kindness you offer a friend.
Key Takeaways
  • The three components of Self-Compassion: Self-kindness, Common Humanity, Mindfulness.
  • Shame shuts down the learning centers of the brain; compassion opens them.
  • Soothing touch: hand on heart can lower cortisol levels.
  • The Inner Critic vs. The Inner Coach: motivating through support rather than fear.

The Physiology of Self-Criticism

When you criticize yourself, your brain perceives a threat. Since you are both the attacker and the attacked, the body goes into a chronic stress state.

Self-compassion releases oxytocin and opiates, which directly counter the stress response. It is the fastest way to feel safe.

Common Humanity: realizing 'I am not alone in this feeling' breaks the isolation of suffering.

Exercise 1

Hand on Heart: When stressed, place your hand firmly on your chest. Feel the warmth. Breathe into your hand for 2 minutes.

The Self-Compassion Break

A 3-step practice for difficult moments:

1. Mindfulness: 'This is a moment of suffering. This is hard.'

2. Common Humanity: 'Suffering is a part of life. Other people feel this too.'

3. Self-Kindness: 'May I be kind to myself. May I give myself what I need.'

Exercise 2

Letter to Self: Write a letter to yourself about a recent mistake, but write it from the perspective of an unconditionally loving friend.

Reparenting the Inner Child

Often, big emotions are old emotions. A small trigger might activate an old wound.

Imagine the part of you that is upset is a child. You wouldn't yell at a scared child. You would comfort them.

Talk to yourself as a wise, loving parent: 'I know this is scary, but I've got you. We can handle this.'
