Module 2 • Somatic Awareness: Bottom-Up Regulation

Overview
  • You cannot think your way out of a feeling; you have to feel your way out. Emotions live in the body.
  • This module focuses on 'Bottom-Up' regulation—using the body to calm the mind. This is often faster and more effective than positive thinking during high stress.
  • Learn to read your body's subtle signals (Interoception) before they become overwhelming storms.
Key Takeaways
  • Interoception is the superpower of regulation: the ability to feel the inside of your body.
  • The Vagus Nerve is the brake pedal: stimulating it signals safety to the brain.
  • Grounding brings you back to the present: anxiety lives in the future; the body lives in the now.
  • Postures of power: your physical stance dictates your emotional state.

The Body-Mind Loop

The brain constantly scans the body to decide how to feel. If your breathing is shallow and your shoulders are tight, your brain assumes there is a threat and produces more anxiety.

By consciously changing your physical state (deepening breath, relaxing muscles), you send a 'safety signal' back up to the brain. This is 'Bottom-Up' regulation.

Cognitive strategies (thinking differently) are often inaccessible when we are highly triggered. Somatic strategies work when logic fails.

Exercise 1

Body Scan: Spend 5 minutes scanning your body from toe to head. Where are you holding tension? Release it consciously.

The Window of Tolerance

Visualise a window. Inside the window, you can handle life's ups and downs. You are calm and engaged.

Above the window is 'Hyper-arousal' (anxiety, anger, panic). Below the window is 'Hypo-arousal' (shutdown, depression, numbness).

Our goal is to widen your window so that stressors don't push you out of it, and to use somatic tools to bring you back in when you exit.

Exercise 2

Box Breathing: Inhale 4 seconds, Hold 4 seconds, Exhale 4 seconds, Hold 4 seconds. Repeat for 2 minutes.

The Breath as a Remote Control

The exhale is the most accessible tool for regulation. Extending your exhale activates the parasympathetic nervous system (rest and digest).

Box Breathing (Navy SEAL technique) and 4-7-8 breathing are mechanical ways to force the body into a calm state.

Homework
  • Grounding Practice: Use the 5-4-3-2-1 technique once a day. (5 things you see, 4 you feel, 3 you hear, 2 you smell, 1 you taste).
  • Posture experiment: Notice your posture when you feel stressed. Deliberately change it (shoulders down, chest open). Note the shift in feeling.
