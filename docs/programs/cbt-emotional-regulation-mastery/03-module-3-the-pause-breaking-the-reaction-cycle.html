Module 3 • The Pause: Breaking the Reaction Cycle

Overview
  • Between stimulus and response, there is a space. In that space is our power to choose our growth and our freedom. — Viktor Frankl.
  • This module teaches you how to widen that space. You will learn the 'STOP' skill from DBT to prevent impulsive reactions that you regret later.
  • We move from 'reacting' (automatic, habit-based) to 'responding' (intentional, values-based).
Key Takeaways
  • Reactivity is a habit: you can rewire the habit of immediate reaction.
  • The STOP skill: Stop, Take a step back, Observe, Proceed mindfully.
  • Urge Surfing: desires to react (yell, send the text, eat) are like waves; they peak and crash. You don't have to act on them.
  • The 'Check the Facts' skill: is your emotion fitting the actual facts of the situation?

The anatomy of a reaction

A reaction is immediate and historical. It's based on past survival patterns. A response is delayed and present-focused. It considers the current context and your long-term goals.

Reactions usually feel satisfying in the moment (venting anger) but costly in the long run (damaged relationships). Responses often feel difficult in the moment (holding your tongue) but rewarding in the long run.

The key to responding is buying time. Even 5 seconds can be enough to get the prefrontal cortex back online.

The STOP Skill (DBT)

S: Stop. Do not move a muscle. Do not speak. Freeze your body.

T: Take a step back. Physically step back or take a deep breath. Detach from the situation.

O: Observe. Notice what you are feeling and thinking. Notice the situation. What is the goal here?

P: Proceed mindfully. Act with awareness. Ask: 'Will this action make the situation better or worse?'

Exercise 2

Urge Surf: Identify one small habit (checking phone, snacking). When the urge hits, set a timer for 3 minutes. Wait. Surf the urge. If you still want to do it after 3 minutes, go ahead.

Urge Surfing

When you feel the urge to react destructively, visualize it as a wave. Most people think the urge will keep rising until they explode.

In reality, urges peak and then subside if you don't feed them. 'Surfing' means watching the urge rise, crest, and fall without acting on it.

This builds 'distress tolerance'—the muscle of being uncomfortable without acting out.

Homework
  • Reaction Audit: At the end of the day, review one time you reacted and one time you responded. What made the difference?
  • Values check: Write down your top 3 values (e.g., Kindness, Integrity, Patience). Keep them visible. Before acting, ask: 'Is this consistent with my values?'
