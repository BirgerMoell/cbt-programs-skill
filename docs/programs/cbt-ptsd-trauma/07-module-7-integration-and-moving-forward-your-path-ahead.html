Your maintenance plan

Recovery from trauma is a journey, not a destination. You'll have good days and difficult days. You'll have periods of progress and periods of challenge. This is normal.

A maintenance plan helps you stay on track. It includes: Daily practices—grounding, emotional regulation, self-compassion, Weekly practices—trauma processing, cognitive restructuring, behavioral activation, Warning signs—what indicates you're struggling, Action plan—what to do when warning signs appear, Supports—people, resources, techniques that help, Values—what matters to you, what you're working toward.

Think of your maintenance plan as a roadmap. When you feel lost, you can refer to it. When you encounter obstacles, you can adjust it. It's a living document that evolves with you.

Exercise 2

Navigating setbacks

Setbacks are normal. You might have a difficult week, a return of symptoms, or a return of old patterns. This doesn't mean you've failed. It means you're human, and recovery isn't linear.

When setbacks occur: Acknowledge them without judgment ('I'm having a difficult time right now'), Review your maintenance plan ('What tools can I use?'), Practice self-compassion ('This is hard, and that's okay'), Take small steps ('What's one thing I can do today?'), Reach out for support ('Who can I talk to?').

Setbacks are opportunities to practice what you've learned. They're not failures—they're part of the journey. How you respond to setbacks matters more than the setbacks themselves.
