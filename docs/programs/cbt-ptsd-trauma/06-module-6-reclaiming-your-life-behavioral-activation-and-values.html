Module 6 • Reclaiming Your Life: Behavioral Activation and Values

Overview
  • Trauma can make you withdraw from life. This module helps you reclaim your life by re-engaging with activities, relationships, and values that matter to you.
  • We explore behavioral activation—gradually re-engaging with activities you've been avoiding. This helps break the cycle of avoidance that maintains PTSD.
  • The module introduces values clarification—identifying what matters to you, what gives your life meaning. Trauma can make you lose sight of your values. Reconnecting with them helps you rebuild meaning.
  • You'll learn to create an activity hierarchy—a list of activities ranked by how important they are to you and how much anxiety they cause. This helps you prioritize and gradually re-engage.
  • We explore the importance of relationships in recovery. Trauma can make you isolate. Reconnecting with safe people is essential for healing.
  • Finally, we introduce the concept of 'post-traumatic growth'—the possibility of growing from trauma, finding meaning, and living a rich, full life despite what happened.
Key Takeaways
  • Behavioral activation helps break the cycle of avoidance.
  • Reconnecting with values helps rebuild meaning.
  • Gradual re-engagement with activities and relationships supports recovery.
  • Post-traumatic growth is possible—you can grow from trauma.

Reclaiming what trauma took

Trauma can take a lot from you—your sense of safety, your trust, your joy, your connection to life itself. This module is about reclaiming what trauma took, about taking back your life.

This doesn't mean pretending trauma didn't happen or 'getting over it.' It means honoring your experience while also choosing to live fully. It means saying: 'This happened, AND I'm still here. I'm still me. I can still live.'

Reclaiming your life involves: Re-engaging with activities you've been avoiding, Reconnecting with values that matter to you, Rebuilding relationships with safe people, Finding meaning and purpose, Living fully despite what happened.

This takes courage. It means facing fears, taking risks, choosing life. But it's also liberating. Every activity you re-engage with, every value you reconnect with, every relationship you rebuild is a victory. It's you saying: 'Trauma doesn't get to define me. I get to define me.'

Exercise 1

Values Identification: Identify your top 5-10 values. Write about why each matters to you. How have you lived these values in the past? How can you live them now?

Behavioral activation

Behavioral activation involves gradually re-engaging with activities you've been avoiding due to trauma. This breaks the cycle of avoidance that maintains PTSD.

Start small. Choose activities that: Matter to you, Feel manageable, Don't trigger overwhelming anxiety. Examples might include: Going for a walk, Calling a friend, Engaging in a hobby, Attending a social event, Pursuing a goal.

Schedule these activities. Put them in your calendar. Commit to them. Even if you don't feel like it, do them anyway. Action comes before motivation.

Notice what happens. Do you feel better? Do you feel more connected? Do you feel more like yourself? These positive experiences reinforce re-engagement, making it easier over time.

Exercise 2

Activity Hierarchy: List activities you've been avoiding. Rank them by importance and anxiety. Start with high-importance, low-anxiety activities.

Values clarification

Values are what matter to you—the principles that guide how you want to live. Trauma can make you lose sight of your values. Reconnecting with them helps you rebuild meaning.

Common values include: Connection, Growth, Contribution, Authenticity, Adventure, Security, Creativity, Health, Family, Meaning, Service, Learning.

Identify your top values. Ask: 'What matters to me? What gives my life meaning? How do I want to live?'

Then, align your actions with your values. Every day, ask: 'What can I do today that aligns with my values?' Even small actions count. Every value-aligned action is a step toward reclaiming your life.

Exercise 3

Behavioral Activation Plan: Choose 3-5 activities from your hierarchy. Schedule them for this week. Commit to doing them, even if you don't feel like it.

Activity hierarchy

An activity hierarchy helps you prioritize re-engagement. List activities you've been avoiding, then rank them by: How important they are to you (values alignment), How much anxiety they cause.

Start with activities that are: High in importance, Low in anxiety. As you build confidence, gradually move to activities that are: High in importance, Higher in anxiety.

This systematic approach helps you re-engage safely, building momentum over time.

Exercise 4

Relationship Reconnection: Identify 2-3 safe people you want to reconnect with. Make a plan to reach out. Start small—a text, a call, a brief meet-up.

Rebuilding relationships

Trauma can make you isolate. You might feel disconnected, unable to trust, afraid of being hurt again. But connection is essential for healing.

Rebuilding relationships involves: Identifying safe people, Starting small—brief, low-pressure interactions, Gradually increasing connection, Setting boundaries that protect you, Being authentic about your experience (when safe), Accepting support.

This takes time. Be patient. Not everyone will be safe. That's okay. Focus on the people who are.

Exercise 5

Post-Traumatic Growth Reflection: Reflect on areas where you might be growing: Appreciation for life, Relationships, Personal strength, New possibilities, Spiritual growth. Write about this.

Post-traumatic growth

Post-traumatic growth is the possibility of growing from trauma. It doesn't mean trauma was good or that you should be grateful for it. It means that despite trauma, you can grow, find meaning, and live a rich, full life.

Common areas of growth include: Appreciation for life, Relationships, Personal strength, New possibilities, Spiritual growth.

This doesn't happen automatically. It requires: Processing trauma, Challenging stuck points, Reconnecting with values, Re-engaging with life, Finding meaning.

You might not see growth yet, and that's okay. Healing takes time. But growth is possible. You can live fully despite what happened.

Homework
  • Complete your Values Identification. Review your values weekly. Ask: 'How am I living my values? What can I do to align more with them?'
  • Create your Activity Hierarchy. Start re-engaging with high-importance, low-anxiety activities. Gradually work your way up.
  • Schedule and complete behavioral activation activities weekly. Notice how re-engagement affects your mood, connection, and sense of self.
  • Reach out to at least one safe person this week. Start rebuilding connections. Notice how it feels.
  • Reflect on post-traumatic growth monthly. Notice areas where you're growing, even if growth is small.
