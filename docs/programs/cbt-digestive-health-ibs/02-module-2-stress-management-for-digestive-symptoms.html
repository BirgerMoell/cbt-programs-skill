Calming the gut through the mind

When you're stressed, your digestive system goes into 'fight or flight' mode. Digestion slows. Muscles tense. Symptoms flare. But you can reverse this.

Through relaxation techniques, you can activate the 'rest and digest' system. You can signal to your gut that it's safe to relax. You can calm symptoms through your mind.

This isn't magic—it's science. The vagus nerve connects your brain to your gut. When you activate it through breathing, relaxation, or imagery, you send calming signals to your digestive system.

The relaxation response

The relaxation response is the opposite of the stress response. Your heart rate slows. Your breathing deepens. Your muscles relax. Your digestive system calms.

You can activate this intentionally. Through deep breathing. Through progressive muscle relaxation. Through guided imagery. Through meditation.

We're going to practice these techniques regularly. Not just when symptoms are bad, but daily. Like medication, they work best when taken consistently.

Gut-directed techniques

Some relaxation techniques are specifically designed for digestive health. Gut-directed hypnotherapy uses imagery to calm the gut. You might imagine warmth spreading through your abdomen, or visualize your digestive system working smoothly.

Diaphragmatic breathing massages the digestive organs and activates the vagus nerve. It's simple but powerful.

We're going to learn these techniques. We're going to practice them. We're going to use them when symptoms flare, and we're going to use them preventively.
