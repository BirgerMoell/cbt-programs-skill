Module 2 • The Neuroscience: Your Brain on Psychedelics

Overview
  • What actually happens physically when you ingest a psychedelic? It's not magic; it's neurochemistry.
  • This module explains the 5-HT2A receptor, the Default Mode Network (DMN), and the concept of neuroplasticity.
  • Understanding the biology helps you surrender to the experience, knowing that the 'chaos' you feel is actually the brain rewiring itself.
Key Takeaways
  • Classic psychedelics primarily act on the Serotonin 2A receptor (5-HT2A).
  • They relax the 'Default Mode Network' (DMN), which is the seat of the ego and rigid thinking patterns.
  • The brain becomes hyper-connected, allowing distinct regions to communicate (synesthesia).
  • Neuroplasticity is increased, opening a 'window of opportunity' for changing habits.

The key: 5-HT2A receptors

Most classic psychedelics (LSD, Psilocybin, DMT) are structurally similar to serotonin. They bind specifically to the 5-HT2A receptor.

When activated, these receptors increase neuronal excitability. Imagine the volume knob on your brain's information processing being turned up.

This leads to an influx of sensory information that the brain usually filters out.

The Default Mode Network (DMN)

The DMN is a network of brain regions active when you are thinking about yourself, the past, or the future. It's the 'Manager' or 'Narrator' of your life.

In depression and anxiety, the DMN is often overactive and rigid (rumination). Psychedelics drastically reduce blood flow to the DMN.

Analogy: Imagine your mind is a snowy hill with deep sled grooves (habits). The DMN ensures you stay in the grooves. Psychedelics are like a fresh snowfall, filling the grooves and allowing you to sled in new directions.

The REBUS Model

REBUS stands for 'Relaxed Beliefs Under Psychedelics.' Our brains are prediction machines. We usually see what we expect to see.

Psychedelics weaken these 'top-down' predictions. Instead of your brain imposing order on reality, you experience raw data from the 'bottom-up'.

This is why you might see a flower 'breathing'—your brain has stopped filtering the raw sensory input with the label 'static object'.
