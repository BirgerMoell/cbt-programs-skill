Ancient roots to modern labs

Long before they were studied in labs, compounds like psilocybin and mescaline were used in structured ceremonies. Indigenous wisdom emphasized reverence, lineage, and structure—elements that modern science is now validating as essential for safety.

In the mid-20th century, psychiatry viewed these compounds as 'psychotomimetics' (mimicking psychosis) before realizing they were actually 'psychedelics' (mind-manifesting). Thousands of clinical papers were published before prohibition shut the doors.

Today, institutions like Johns Hopkins and Imperial College London are proving that under the right conditions, these experiences can treat depression, PTSD, and addiction effectively.

Defining 'Psychedelic'

The word comes from the Greek 'psyche' (mind/soul) and 'deloun' (to make visible). These substances don't just add hallucinations; they reveal the subconscious mind.

Unlike alcohol or opiates which numb or suppress, psychedelics amplify. This amplification is why safety protocols are non-negotiable.

We approach this topic with 'Epistemic Humility'—acknowledging that while science can explain the chemistry, the subjective experience remains a profound mystery.
