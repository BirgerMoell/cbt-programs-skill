Module 1 • Reframing the Rush

Overview
  • We normalize the fear: Glossophobia is one of the most common fears. The body perceives the audience as a 'predator pack.'
  • We reframe the symptoms: A racing heart is not a heart attack; it is 'energy.' It is the body preparing you to perform.
  • We distinguish between 'Anxiety' (fear of failure) and 'Excitement' (readiness). Physiologically, they are almost identical.
  • We introduce 'The Pause.' Learning to stand in silence before speaking to ground the nervous system.
Key Takeaways
  • Adrenaline is fuel; don't try to get rid of it, use it.
  • The audience wants you to succeed; they are not waiting for you to die.
  • You don't need to be calm to be effective; you just need to be brave.

It's not fear, it's fuel

Your hands shake. Your mouth is dry. Your heart pounds. You think, 'I'm terrified.' But what if you thought, 'I'm ready'?

Top athletes feel the exact same sensations before a game. They call it 'being in the zone.' You call it 'panic.' The sensation is the same; the label is different. We are going to change the label.

Exercise 1

The Re-labeling Drill: Run in place for 1 minute to get your heart rate up. Say out loud: 'I am excited! I am ready!'
