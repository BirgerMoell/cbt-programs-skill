Module 3 • Rewiring the Mind: Grief and Cognitive Adaptation

Overview
  • MS involves loss. Loss of mobility, loss of certainty, loss of the 'old you.' It is crucial to grieve, but dangerous to get stuck in the grief.
  • This module applies Cognitive Restructuring to the dark thoughts that come with chronic illness: 'I am a burden,' 'I am broken,' 'My life is over.'
  • We fight the neurological damage with psychological neuroplasticity. We cannot fix the myelin, but we can upgrade the mindset.
Key Takeaways
  • Grief is not linear; it is a spiral. Allow yourself to feel it.
  • Thoughts are not facts. 'I can't do this' is a feeling, not a prophecy.
  • Focus on 'Can Do' vs. 'Can't Do' without toxic positivity.

Confronting the 'I am Broken' narrative

When your leg drags or your hand shakes, the automatic thought is often 'I am broken.' This thought triggers depression, which worsens fatigue.

We need to catch that thought and reframe it. You are not broken; you are *adapting*. You are navigating a complex challenge. That makes you resilient, not defective.

The grief of the 'Old Self'

It is okay to miss the version of you that could run a marathon or stay up all night. But staring at the closed door prevents you from seeing the open window.

We will work on 'Integration'—merging the old you with the new reality to create a version of yourself that is different, but deeper.

Exercises
  • The Thought Record: Catch a negative thought (e.g., 'I am useless'). Write down the evidence *against* it (e.g., 'I listened to my friend today, I finished a project').
  • Values Alignment: List 3 core values (e.g., Kindness, Creativity). List how you can express them *even with* MS symptoms.
Homework
  • Grief Letter: Write a letter to your diagnosis. Scream at it, cry at it, thank it for the lessons. Get it all out on paper.
