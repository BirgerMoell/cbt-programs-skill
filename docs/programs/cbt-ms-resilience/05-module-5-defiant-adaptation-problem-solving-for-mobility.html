Module 5 • Defiant Adaptation: Problem-Solving for Mobility

Overview
  • Adaptation is not surrender. Adaptation is defiance. It is saying, 'I will do this, even if I have to do it differently.'
  • This module uses the 'Selection, Optimization, and Compensation' (SOC) model. We tackle physical limitations with a problem-solving engineer's mindset.
  • We address the shame of using assistive devices (canes, scooters) and reframe them as tools of liberation.
Key Takeaways
  • A cane is not a symbol of weakness; it is a tool for freedom.
  • Doing it differently is better than not doing it at all.
  • Focus on the *function*, not the *method*.

The Engineer's Mindset

If a road is blocked, you don't cancel the trip; you find a detour. MS blocks certain neural roads. Your job is to find the detour.

If you can't stand to cook dinner, sit on a stool. If you can't hold a pen, use voice-to-text. This isn't failing; this is outsmarting the disease.

Reframing Assistive Tech

Society tells us that needing a wheelchair is a tragedy. We say: If the wheelchair allows you to go to the museum with your kids, the wheelchair is a miracle.

We will work on the internal shame that stops you from using the tools that would give you your life back.

Exercises
  • The Obstacle Course: Pick one activity you have stopped doing (e.g., gardening). Brainstorm 3 ways to adapt it so you can do it (e.g., raised beds, sitting, early morning).
  • Tool Test: Go to a medical supply store or look online. Find one tool that would make your life 10% easier. Commit to trying it.
Homework
  • The 'Yes, And' Experiment: Do an activity differently. Tell yourself, 'I am doing this differently, AND I am enjoying it.'
