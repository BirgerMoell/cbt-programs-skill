Module 3 • Managing Cravings & Withdrawal

Overview

Key Takeaways
  • Cravings are temporary—they peak and then subside, usually within 5-10 minutes.
  • Urge surfing—observing without acting—reduces craving power.
  • Withdrawal symptoms are temporary and manageable.
  • Distraction and alternative activities can reduce craving intensity.

The craving wave

When you quit, cravings will come. They'll feel urgent. 'I need a cigarette now.' But here's what you need to know: Cravings are waves. They build, peak, and then subside.

Most cravings last 5-10 minutes. If you can ride them out—if you can surf the wave without acting—they pass. Each time you do this, cravings get weaker. Your confidence grows.

We're going to teach you to surf. To observe the craving. To breathe through it. To distract yourself. To wait it out. Because it will pass.

Exercise 1

The Craving Observation Practice: Next time you have a craving (before or after quitting), observe it. Where do you feel it? What thoughts come? How long does it last? Practice urge surfing.

Understanding withdrawal

When you quit, your body adjusts. It's used to nicotine. Without it, you might feel irritable, anxious, restless, foggy. These are withdrawal symptoms. They're normal. They're temporary.

Withdrawal peaks in the first few days, then gradually decreases. Most physical symptoms are gone within a week. Psychological cravings can last longer, but they get weaker.

Knowing what to expect helps. You're not going crazy. You're not weak. You're experiencing normal withdrawal. And it will pass.

Riding it out

The key to managing cravings is not fighting them, but riding them out. Like a wave, you can't stop it, but you can surf it.

Urge surfing means noticing the craving. Feeling it in your body. Observing your thoughts. Breathing. Waiting. Knowing it will pass.

You can also distract yourself. Engage in an activity. Move your body. Call someone. Do something with your hands. Cravings lose power when your attention is elsewhere.

Homework
  • Practice urge surfing with current cravings (if still smoking) or past cravings (if quit). Notice how observing changes the experience.
  • Use distraction techniques when cravings hit. Try different activities. Notice which are most effective for you.
  • If experiencing withdrawal, track symptoms. Notice patterns: When are they worst? What helps? Remember: They're temporary.
