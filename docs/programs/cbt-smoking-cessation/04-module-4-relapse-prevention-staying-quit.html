Module 4 • Relapse Prevention: Staying Quit

Overview
  • This module focuses on the critical skill of relapse prevention—maintaining your quit long-term.
  • We explore 'The Relapse Process'—understanding that relapse is usually a process, not a single event, and learning to recognize warning signs.
  • You will learn 'High-Risk Situations'—identifying situations that increase relapse risk and creating specific plans for managing them.
  • The concept of 'Lapse vs. Relapse' is introduced. A lapse (one cigarette) doesn't have to become a relapse (return to regular smoking).
  • We address 'Cognitive Factors in Relapse'—thoughts that can lead to smoking ('just one won't hurt,' 'I've already failed') and how to challenge them.
  • You will practice 'Problem-Solving for Challenges'—systematic approaches to addressing obstacles that arise.
  • Finally, we create a 'Relapse Prevention Plan'—a personalized system for maintaining your quit long-term.
Key Takeaways
  • Relapse is usually a process with warning signs—recognizing them early helps prevent relapse.
  • High-risk situations are predictable—having a plan prevents impulsive smoking.
  • A lapse doesn't have to become a relapse—how you respond matters.
  • Challenging unhelpful thoughts prevents cognitive relapse.

The relapse process

Relapse doesn't usually happen suddenly. It's a process. First, you might stop using your strategies. Then, you might start thinking about smoking. Then, you might romanticize it. Then, you might have 'just one.'

But you can interrupt this process. If you recognize warning signs early, you can intervene. You can return to your strategies. You can prevent a lapse from becoming a relapse.

We're going to teach you to recognize warning signs. We're going to help you create a plan. We're going to build your relapse prevention skills.

High-risk situations

Some situations are riskier than others. Stressful events. Social situations with smokers. Alcohol. Emotional states. These are your high-risk situations.

Knowing your risks helps you prepare. You can avoid some situations early in your quit. For others, you can create specific plans. You can have strategies ready.

We're going to identify your high-risk situations. We're going to create plans for each. We're going to prepare you for challenges.

Identify Your High-Risk Situations: List 5-7 situations that increase your risk of smoking. For each, create a specific prevention plan.

Lapse vs. relapse

If you smoke one cigarette, that's a lapse. It's a mistake. It's not ideal, but it doesn't have to be the end.

A relapse is returning to regular smoking. That's what we want to prevent.

The difference? How you respond. If you have a lapse and think 'I've failed, I might as well keep smoking,' that's a relapse. If you think 'That was a mistake, I'm getting back on track,' that's recovery.

We're going to prepare you for lapses. We're going to help you respond effectively. We're going to prevent lapses from becoming relapses.

Homework
  • Review your Warning Signs Checklist weekly. If you notice any signs, take action immediately. Return to your strategies.
  • Practice your High-Risk Situation plans. Visualize yourself in these situations, using your strategies successfully.
  • If you have a lapse, use your Lapse Response Plan. Don't let one cigarette become a relapse. Get back on track immediately.
