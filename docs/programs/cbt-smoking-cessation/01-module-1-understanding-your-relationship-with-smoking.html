CBT Program

Module 1 • Understanding Your Relationship with Smoking

Overview
  • We begin by exploring your personal history with smoking. When did you start? Why? What role does smoking play in your life now?
  • You will learn about 'Nicotine Dependence'—how nicotine affects your brain and creates physical and psychological dependence.
  • We explore 'The Functions of Smoking'—what smoking does for you (stress relief, social connection, habit, etc.). Understanding these functions helps you find alternatives.
  • The concept of 'Smoking Triggers' is introduced—situations, emotions, or thoughts that cue the urge to smoke.
  • You will complete a 'Smoking Pattern Assessment'—tracking when, where, why, and with whom you smoke to identify patterns.
  • We address 'Motivation & Readiness'—assessing your readiness to quit and strengthening your motivation.
  • Finally, we set the foundation: Quitting smoking is a process, not an event. It requires preparation, support, and persistence.
Key Takeaways
  • Understanding why you smoke helps you quit effectively.
  • Smoking serves multiple functions; you'll need alternatives for each.
  • Triggers are predictable; knowing them helps you prepare.
  • Readiness and motivation are key to successful quitting.

Your smoking story

Every smoker has a story. Maybe you started as a teenager, trying to fit in. Maybe you started during a stressful time, seeking relief. Maybe you can't even remember why—it just became part of who you are.

Smoking isn't just a habit—it's a relationship. It's been there through stress, celebrations, boredom, and routine. It's served functions: stress relief, social connection, time management, emotional regulation.

To quit successfully, you need to understand this relationship. What does smoking do for you? What will you need instead? This understanding is the foundation of change.

The nicotine trap

Nicotine is one of the most addictive substances. It reaches your brain in seconds, releasing dopamine—the 'feel good' chemical. Your brain learns: 'Smoking = pleasure.'

But here's the trap: The pleasure is temporary. Soon, you need nicotine just to feel normal. Without it, you feel anxious, irritable, restless. So you smoke again. The cycle continues.

Understanding this cycle helps. You're not weak-willed. You're dealing with a powerful addiction. But addiction can be overcome. Millions of people have quit. You can too.

Why now?

Why do you want to quit? Health? Money? Family? Freedom? Your reasons matter. They're your motivation. They're what will carry you through cravings and challenges.

But motivation alone isn't enough. You also need readiness. Are you ready to face withdrawal? To change routines? To find new ways to cope? Readiness is about being prepared for the process.

We're going to strengthen both. We're going to clarify your reasons. We're going to prepare you for the journey. We're going to build your confidence.

Homework
  • Continue your Smoking Pattern Log for 4 more days. The longer you track, the clearer patterns become.
  • List Your Reasons to Quit: Write down all reasons you want to quit. Be specific. Review this list daily. Add to it as you think of more reasons.
  • Assess Your Readiness: On a scale of 1-10, how ready are you to quit? What would need to change to increase your readiness?
