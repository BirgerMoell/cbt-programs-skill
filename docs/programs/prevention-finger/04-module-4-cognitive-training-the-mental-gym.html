Module 4 • Cognitive Training: The Mental Gym

Overview
  • Using it or losing it. The intervention group used computer-based training for memory and speed.
  • We target specific domains: Executive Function (planning/organizing), Psychomotor Speed (reaction time), and Complex Memory.
  • Learning to embrace the frustration of learning. If it feels easy, it's not training.
  • Moving beyond passive activities (like watching TV) to active engagement (puzzles, languages, strategy games).
Key Takeaways
  • The brain grows when challenged.
  • Executive function (the CEO of the brain) showed the biggest improvement in the study.
  • Variety is key; don't just do one type of puzzle.

Lifting mental weights

Doing the crossword every day is good, but if you're good at crosswords, it's no longer a workout. It's just recall.

To build cognitive reserve, you need novelty and difficulty. You need to do things that make you feel a little slow, a little clumsy. That feeling of 'effort' is the feeling of neuroplasticity happening.

Exercise 1

Speed Drill: Take a deck of cards. Sort them into suits as fast as you possibly can. Time yourself. Try to beat your time tomorrow.

Homework
  • Engage in a 'Complex Memory' task daily (e.g., memorize a grocery list and leave the paper at home).
  • Spend 15 minutes learning a new skill (language, instrument, or game).
