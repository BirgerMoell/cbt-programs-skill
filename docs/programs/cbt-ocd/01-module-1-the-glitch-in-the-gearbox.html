CBT Program

Module 1 • The Glitch in the Gearbox

Overview
  • We begin by externalizing OCD. It is not a character flaw; it is a mechanical glitch in the brain's warning system. We map the OCD Cycle: Obsession (Trigger) → Anxiety (Distress) → Compulsion (Ritual) → Temporary Relief.
  • Psycho-education focuses on 'Intrusive Thoughts.' Everyone has weird thoughts; the OCD brain just gets 'sticky' on them, interpreting them as significant or dangerous.
  • We introduce the concept of 'Negative Reinforcement.' You will learn how performing a ritual (checking, washing, ruminating) momentarily lowers anxiety but guarantees the anxiety will return stronger next time.
  • We distinguish between 'Ego-Syntonic' (thoughts that align with your values) and 'Ego-Dystonic' (thoughts that horrify you). OCD thoughts are almost always the latter.
  • You will start a 'Ritual Log.' Tracking not just the trigger, but the specific compulsion you performed and how long the relief lasted.
  • We introduce the core philosophy of recovery: 'The goal is not to stop the thoughts; the goal is to stop the reaction.'
  • Finally, we prepare for the work ahead. We are going to learn to sit with anxiety without fixing it. This is hard, but it is the only way out.
Key Takeaways
  • Compulsions provide short-term relief but fuel long-term suffering.
  • Intrusive thoughts are junk mail; your brain just keeps marking them 'Urgent.'
  • You cannot control the thought (Obsession), but you can control the action (Compulsion).
  • The content of the obsession matters less than the cycle itself.

The sticky brain

Imagine your brain is an email inbox. Most people have a spam filter that automatically deletes weird, violent, or nonsensical thoughts. In OCD, the spam filter is broken. Every thought is marked 'URGENT' and 'IMPORTANT.'

You are not a bad person for having these thoughts. In fact, OCD tends to attack the things you care about most. If you love your family, OCD threatens them. If you value morality, OCD suggests you are a sinner. It uses your values against you.

This module is about recognizing the glitch. When the 'Check Engine' light comes on for the 100th time today, we are going to learn to see it as a broken sensor, not a broken car.

The trap of relief

Anxiety spikes. You feel you *must* check the stove, or wash your hands, or review a conversation in your head. You do it. The anxiety drops. 'Phew,' you think. 'I’m safe.'

This is the trap. By performing the ritual, you have taught your brain: 'The danger was real, and the ritual saved me.' The brain learns that it needs the ritual to survive. Next time, the urge will be stronger. We are going to break this loop.

Junk Mail Labeling: For one day, whenever an intrusive thought appears, visualize stamping it with a big red 'SPAM' stamp. Do not open the email, just stamp it.

  • Identify your 'Core Fear'—what is the terrible thing you believe will happen if you don't do the ritual?
