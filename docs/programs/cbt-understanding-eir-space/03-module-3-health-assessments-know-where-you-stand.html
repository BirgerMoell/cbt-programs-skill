Module 3 • Health Assessments: Know Where You Stand

Overview
  • How do you know if something is a problem worth addressing? How do you track whether you're improving? Health Assessments give you clear, structured ways to understand your current health status.
  • These aren't random quizzes—they're validated instruments used by healthcare professionals. By taking assessments and tracking results over time, you get objective measures of your progress.
  • Assessment results help you focus your efforts where they matter most and give you concrete evidence to share with your care team.
Key Takeaways
  • Assessments provide objective measures of your health status in specific areas.
  • Tracking assessment results over time shows whether you're improving.
  • Assessment results help you prioritize what to work on.
  • You can share results with your healthcare providers for better care.

Beyond guessing: knowing where you stand

We often have a vague sense of how we're doing—'I've been stressed lately' or 'My mood seems off.' But vague feelings are hard to act on. How stressed? Compared to when? Getting better or worse?

Health Assessments turn vague feelings into concrete information. They ask structured questions and give you scores you can track over time. Now you know: 'My anxiety score dropped from 15 to 9 over three months.'

This isn't about labeling yourself or obsessing over numbers. It's about having clear information to guide your efforts and measure your progress.

What you can assess

EIR Space includes assessments for many health dimensions: mental health (anxiety, depression, stress), physical wellbeing, sleep quality, social connection, specific conditions, and more.

Each assessment is based on validated instruments—the same ones used in clinical settings. Your results are meaningful and comparable over time.

Choose assessments that matter to you. If you're concerned about stress, take a stress assessment. If sleep is your priority, assess sleep quality. Let your concerns guide your choices.

Using your results

Assessment results tell you two things: where you stand now and whether you're improving. Both are valuable.

If an assessment reveals a concern, you know where to focus. Maybe you need a Health Program for anxiety. Maybe you should discuss something with your doctor. The assessment points the way.

If results show improvement, you have evidence that your efforts are working. This is motivating—and useful when talking to healthcare providers about your progress.

Making assessments work for you

Here's a simple practice: Take a few baseline assessments when you start using EIR Space. Then retake them monthly or quarterly. This gives you a clear picture of your health trajectory.

Before doctor appointments, consider taking relevant assessments. Showing your doctor concrete trends is much more useful than saying 'I feel a bit better.'

Remember: assessments are tools, not judgments. A high anxiety score isn't a failure—it's information that helps you take action.

Homework
  • Comprehensive baseline: Take assessments in each major category (mental health, physical wellbeing, etc.) to establish baselines.
  • Assessment schedule: Set a reminder to retake assessments in one month. Compare results to your baselines.
  • Doctor sharing: If you have an upcoming appointment, bring relevant assessment results to discuss with your provider.
  • Focus selection: Based on your assessments, identify one area to prioritize over the next month.
