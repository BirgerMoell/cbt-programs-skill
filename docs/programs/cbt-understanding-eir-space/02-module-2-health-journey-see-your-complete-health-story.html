Why your health records matter

Your clinical records contain crucial information: diagnoses, medications, test results, doctor's notes. But scattered across different providers and visits, this information is hard to use.

When you bring your records into EIR Space (from 1177 using BankID, or by uploading an EIR file), you can finally see the big picture. All your records, searchable and organized by date, provider, or category.

This isn't just convenient—it's empowering. When you understand your medical history, you can ask better questions, catch inconsistencies, and participate actively in your care decisions.

Your personal health journal

Clinical records capture what doctors observe. But they miss something crucial: how you actually feel. Your energy levels, your mood, your symptoms between appointments, your daily experience of living in your body.

The health journal is your space to capture all of this. Write freely about how you're feeling. Rate your wellbeing. Track symptoms. Note what helped and what didn't. Use voice dictation when typing feels like too much.

Over time, your journal becomes invaluable. You'll see patterns: 'I always feel worse after poor sleep.' 'My mood improved when I started exercising.' 'That medication helped more than I realized.' These insights can transform your health.

Tracking specific health areas

Sometimes you need to track specific things: sleep, hydration, activity, digestive health. The journal includes specialized tracking for each of these, with interfaces designed to make logging quick and easy.

Why track these things? Because small changes in sleep, hydration, and activity have outsized effects on how you feel. Tracking helps you see the connection between what you do and how you feel.

You don't need to track everything. Choose one or two areas that matter to you. Even simple tracking reveals patterns that can dramatically improve your daily life.

Putting it all together

Your clinical records plus your personal journal create something powerful: a complete health story. The objective (what doctors measured) combined with the subjective (how you experienced it).

This complete picture is what you bring to doctor appointments. It's what the AI can analyze for patterns. It's what you review when making health decisions. It's your health, documented and understood.

To get started: Load your clinical records (if you have them) and try creating your first journal entry. Just write about how you're feeling today. That's all it takes to begin.

Homework
  • Record exploration: Spend 15 minutes exploring your health records. Filter by category or provider. What do you learn?
  • Journal week: Commit to journaling daily for one week—even just a sentence or two. At the end, look back. What patterns emerge?
  • Specific tracking: Choose one area to track (sleep, hydration, or activity) for a week. Notice how tracking affects your awareness.
  • Doctor prep: Before your next appointment, review your records and recent journal entries. What do you want to discuss?
