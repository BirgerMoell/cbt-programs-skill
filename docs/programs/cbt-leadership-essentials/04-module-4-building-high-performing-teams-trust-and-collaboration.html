Module 4 • Building High-Performing Teams: Trust and Collaboration

Overview
  • Great leaders build great teams. But teams don't just happen—they're built intentionally through trust, clear goals, effective processes, and strong relationships.
  • This module teaches you to build teams that are more than the sum of their parts. You'll learn to create psychological safety, establish team norms, facilitate collaboration, and resolve conflicts.
  • Team building isn't about team outings—it's about creating conditions where people can do their best work together.
Key Takeaways
  • Trust is the foundation: without trust, teams can't function effectively.
  • Psychological safety enables performance: people need to feel safe to take risks, admit mistakes, and share ideas.
  • Clear goals and roles prevent conflict: ambiguity creates problems.
  • Great teams have great processes: how you work together matters as much as what you work on.

The foundation of trust

Trust is the foundation of every great team. Without trust, people don't share information, don't take risks, and don't collaborate effectively.

Trust is built through: Consistency (doing what you say you'll do), Competence (demonstrating capability), Care (showing genuine concern for others), and Character (acting with integrity).

Trust takes time to build and moments to break. Be intentional about building it. Protect it fiercely.

As a leader, you set the tone. When you're trustworthy, you create permission for others to be trustworthy too.

Exercise 1

Trust assessment: Rate trust in your team 1–10. What's working? What's missing? What can you do to build more trust?

Creating psychological safety

Psychological safety is the belief that you can speak up without fear of negative consequences. It's essential for high performance.

Teams with psychological safety: share ideas freely, admit mistakes openly, ask questions without fear, challenge each other constructively, and learn from failures.

You create psychological safety by: Admitting your own mistakes, Asking for feedback, Responding non-defensively, Acknowledging uncertainty, and Celebrating learning from failures.

When people feel psychologically safe, they perform better. They're more creative, more engaged, and more likely to take calculated risks.

Exercise 2

Psychological safety audit: How psychologically safe is your team? Can people speak up? Admit mistakes? Ask questions? What would make it safer?

Establishing team norms

Team norms are the unwritten rules that govern how the team works together. Great teams have explicit norms that everyone agrees to.

Common team norms include: How we communicate (channels, response times, meeting etiquette), How we make decisions (consensus, voting, leader decides), How we handle conflict (direct conversation, escalation process), How we celebrate (acknowledgments, recognition), and How we learn (retrospectives, feedback loops).

Establish norms together. Don't impose them. When the team creates them, they own them. Review norms regularly. Update as needed.

Norms prevent problems. When everyone knows how things work, there's less confusion, less conflict, and more focus on the work.

Exercise 3

Team norms creation: Facilitate a conversation to establish or review team norms. Get team input. Document agreements.

Facilitating collaboration

Collaboration doesn't happen automatically. It requires: Clear shared goals (everyone knows what we're trying to achieve), Defined roles (who does what, how we work together), Effective processes (how we make decisions, solve problems, communicate), and Strong relationships (people know and trust each other).

As a leader, your job is to create these conditions. You don't need to be in every conversation, but you need to set up the structures that enable collaboration.

Facilitate, don't dominate. Create space for others to contribute. Ask questions. Synthesize ideas. Make decisions when needed.

Great collaboration feels effortless because the conditions are right. Your job is to create those conditions.

Resolving team conflicts

Conflict is inevitable in teams. The question isn't whether you'll have conflict—it's how you'll handle it.

Healthy conflict (disagreement about ideas) is productive. Unhealthy conflict (personal attacks, hidden agendas) is destructive.

When conflict arises: Address it quickly (don't let it fester), Focus on the issue (not the person), Seek to understand (both perspectives), Find common ground (shared goals, values), and Work together on solutions (collaborative, not competitive).

Sometimes you need to mediate. Sometimes you need to make a decision. Sometimes you need to escalate. Know when to do what.
