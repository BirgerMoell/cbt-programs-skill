Module 7 • Personal Leadership Development: Becoming the Leader You Want to Be

Overview
  • This final module focuses on your personal development as a leader. Leadership isn't just about skills—it's about who you are, how you show up, and the impact you create.
  • You'll develop your leadership philosophy, build self-awareness, create a development plan, and commit to practices that ensure continued growth.
  • Great leaders are always learning. This module gives you the tools to continue growing throughout your leadership journey.
Key Takeaways
  • Leadership is personal: your style should be authentic to you while incorporating best practices.
  • Self-awareness is foundational: you can't lead others well if you don't know yourself.
  • Continuous learning is essential: leadership mastery is a journey, not a destination.
  • Your development plan is your roadmap: use it to guide your growth.

Developing your leadership philosophy

Every great leader has a leadership philosophy—a set of beliefs about what leadership means and how to do it well. Your philosophy guides your decisions, shapes your behavior, and defines your impact.

Your philosophy might include: What leadership means to you, What you value most, How you want to be remembered, What you believe about people, and What you believe about success.

Your philosophy should be: Authentic (true to who you are), Clear (you can articulate it), Actionable (it guides your behavior), and Evolving (it grows as you do).

Write your philosophy. Refine it. Live it. It's your north star.

Building self-awareness

Self-awareness is the foundation of effective leadership. You can't lead others well if you don't know yourself: your strengths, your weaknesses, your triggers, your patterns.

Build self-awareness through: Reflection (regular time to think about your leadership), Feedback (seek it regularly, from multiple sources), Assessment (use tools, but don't over-rely on them), and Observation (notice your patterns, reactions, behaviors).

Self-awareness isn't about being perfect—it's about knowing yourself honestly. When you know yourself, you can work with your strengths and manage your weaknesses.

Regular self-reflection is essential. Set aside time weekly, monthly, quarterly. Review your leadership. What's working? What's not? What are you learning?

Creating your leadership development plan

A development plan helps you focus your growth. It includes: Strengths to leverage (what you do well), Skills to develop (what you're working on), Practices to maintain (habits that support growth), and Metrics to track (how you'll measure progress).

Your plan should be: Specific (clear goals and actions), Realistic (achievable with effort), Flexible (adjustable as you learn), and Sustainable (practices you can maintain).

Review your plan regularly: monthly check-ins, quarterly reviews, annual assessments. Adjust based on what you learn.

Your plan is a living document. Update it as you grow. What worked? What didn't? What's next?

Building leadership habits

Excellence comes from consistent practice. Build habits that support leadership mastery.

Daily habits: Reflect on your leadership (what went well? what didn't?), Practice active listening (in every conversation), Give feedback (when appropriate), and Show appreciation (acknowledge others' contributions).

Weekly habits: Review your team's progress (what's working? what's not?), Have one-on-ones (build relationships, provide support), Practice delegation (develop others, free your time), and Seek feedback (how are you doing?).

Monthly habits: Review your development plan (progress, adjustments), Facilitate team retrospectives (what are we learning?), Assess team health (trust, psychological safety, collaboration), and Plan for the future (what's coming? how do we prepare?).

Habits compound. Small daily practices create significant growth over time.

Seeking support and mentorship

You don't have to develop leadership skills alone. A support system accelerates growth.

Your support system includes: Mentors (experienced leaders who can guide you), Peers (other leaders you can learn with), Coaches (professional development support), and Resources (books, courses, communities).

Ask for feedback regularly: from your manager, your team, your peers. Specific feedback helps you improve faster.

Find a mentor: someone whose leadership you admire, who's willing to invest in you, who can provide perspective and guidance.

Your support system is an investment. Build it intentionally. Maintain it consistently.

Your leadership legacy

How you lead creates your legacy. People remember how you made them feel, how you developed them, how you handled challenges, and how you showed up consistently.

Your leadership legacy includes: The people you develop (their growth and success), The teams you build (their performance and culture), The decisions you make (their impact and outcomes), and The example you set (your character and values).

Think about the leader you want to be. What impact do you want to have? How do you want people to experience you? Work toward that vision.

Your leadership journey doesn't end here. It continues throughout your career. Keep learning. Keep practicing. Keep growing. The best leaders are always students of leadership.
