Module 3 • Coaching for Growth: Developing Your Team

Overview
  • Great leaders are great coaches. They don't just tell people what to do—they help people discover how to do it better themselves.
  • Coaching is different from managing. Managing is about getting work done. Coaching is about developing capability. You need both, but coaching is what creates lasting impact.
  • This module teaches you the fundamentals of coaching: asking powerful questions, active listening, giving feedback, and creating development plans.
Key Takeaways
  • Coaching develops capability: when you coach, you're building skills that last beyond the current task.
  • Questions are more powerful than answers: great coaches ask questions that help people discover solutions.
  • Feedback is a gift: when given well, it accelerates growth and builds trust.
  • Development is ongoing: coaching isn't a one-time event—it's a continuous practice.

Why coaching matters

As a leader, you have two jobs: get work done (managing) and develop people (coaching). Most leaders focus on managing and neglect coaching. But coaching is what creates lasting impact.

When you coach, you're not just solving today's problem—you're building capability for tomorrow. You're teaching people to think, to solve problems, to make decisions. That capability compounds.

Great coaches don't have all the answers. They have great questions. They help people discover their own solutions, which builds confidence and capability.

Coaching is also how you build relationships. When people feel you're invested in their growth, they're more engaged, more committed, and more likely to stay.

The coaching mindset

Coaching requires a specific mindset: Curiosity (genuine interest in understanding), Patience (growth takes time), Belief (people can grow and improve), and Humility (you don't have all the answers).

The coaching mindset is different from the expert mindset. Experts tell. Coaches ask. Experts solve. Coaches help others solve. Experts have answers. Coaches have questions.

This doesn't mean you never give answers. Sometimes people need direction. But default to coaching—ask questions first, provide answers when needed.

The GROW model: a coaching framework

The GROW model is a simple, powerful coaching framework: Goal (what do they want to achieve?), Reality (what's the current situation?), Options (what are the possible approaches?), and Will (what will they commit to doing?).

GROW works because it's structured but flexible. It guides the conversation without being rigid. You can use it in 10 minutes or an hour.

The key is to ask questions, not provide answers. 'What do you think your options are?' is better than 'You should do X.'

Practice GROW regularly. Start with low-stakes situations. Build the skill. It becomes natural with practice.

Giving effective feedback

Feedback is essential for growth, but most people give it poorly. Great feedback is: Specific (concrete examples, not generalizations), Timely (close to the event), Actionable (what to do differently), and Balanced (acknowledge what's working).

The SBI framework helps: Situation (when and where), Behavior (what they did), Impact (how it affected you, the team, or the work).

Example: 'In yesterday's team meeting (Situation), you interrupted Sarah three times (Behavior). This made it hard for her to finish her thoughts and seemed dismissive to the team (Impact).'

After giving feedback, ask for their perspective. 'What's your take on this?' Then work together on solutions.

Creating development plans

Development plans turn coaching into action. A good development plan includes: Current state (where are they now?), Desired state (where do they want to be?), Gap (what's the difference?), Actions (what will they do to close the gap?), and Support (how will you help?).

Development plans should be: Specific (clear actions, not vague goals), Realistic (achievable with effort), and Owned (they create it, you support it).

Review development plans regularly: monthly check-ins, quarterly reviews, annual assessments. Adjust as needed. Celebrate progress.
