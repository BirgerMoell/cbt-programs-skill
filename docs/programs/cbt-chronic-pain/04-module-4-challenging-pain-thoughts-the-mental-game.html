Module 4 • Challenging Pain Thoughts: The Mental Game

Overview
  • This module tackles the cognitive side of the Gate Control Theory. Specifically, 'Catastrophizing'—the belief that the pain is unbearable and will never end.
  • We identify 'Automatic Negative Thoughts' (ANTs) about pain. e.g., 'I am damaged,' 'My life is over,' 'I am a burden.'
  • We use the 'Thought Record' to challenge these thoughts. Examining the evidence: 'Is it true my life is over? Or is it just different?'
  • We address 'Labeling.' How words like 'agony,' 'stabbing,' and 'killing me' can actually intensify the neural signal.
  • The concept of 'Self-Efficacy' is built. Shifting from 'I can't do anything because of pain' to 'I can do some things despite pain.'
  • We explore identity beyond pain. You are a person *with* pain, not a 'pain patient.'
  • Finally, we create 'Coping Statements.' Powerful, believable mantras to use during flare-ups.
Key Takeaways
  • Catastrophizing is one of the strongest predictors of pain intensity; changing it reduces pain.
  • Your language shapes your experience; using neutral words can lower the threat level.
  • You can feel helpless and be capable at the same time.
  • You are more than your MRI scan.

The internal news anchor

Imagine a news anchor in your head reporting on your body. 'Disaster in the lower back! Crisis in the neck! Situation Critical!'

This constant breaking news keeps your brain in high alert. It keeps the pain gate wide open. We want to change the news anchor to a calm, factual reporter.

Instead of 'My back is killing me,' the reporter says, 'There is a strong sensation of tightness in the lower back right now.'

This lowers the emotional temperature. It tells the brain: 'This is unpleasant, but it is not a catastrophe.' The lower the threat, the lower the pain.

Exercise 1

Catastrophe Catching: Catch yourself thinking 'This is terrible' or 'It never ends.' Stop. Take a breath. Replace it with: 'This is a flare-up. I have handled them before. It will pass.'

I am a burden

One of the heaviest thoughts in chronic pain is guilt. 'I am useless,' 'I hold my family back.' These thoughts are heavy. They cause depression, which opens the pain gate.

We need to challenge this. Your value is not defined by how many chores you do. Your value is inherent. You bring love, wisdom, and presence to your relationships. You are worthy of care.

Exercise 2

The Language Swap: Write down 3 intense words you use for pain (e.g., stabbing, burning). Swap them for neutral descriptors (e.g., sharp, warm, intense). Use these for a week.

  • The Language Swap: Write down 3 intense words you use for pain (e.g., stabbing, burning). Swap them for neutral descriptors (e.g., sharp, warm, intense). Use these for a week.
  • Evidence Log: Write down 3 things you accomplished this week despite the pain. Prove to your brain that you are capable.
Homework
  • Complete a Thought Record for a moment of high pain distress.
  • Use your Coping Statements (e.g., 'I am safe,' 'This is temporary') during difficult moments.
  • Read a book or watch a movie that has nothing to do with health—give your brain a break from the topic.
