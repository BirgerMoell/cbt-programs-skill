Module 5 • Building Trust: Learning to Depend on Others

Overview
  • Trust is difficult when you have avoidant attachment. You've learned that depending on others is unsafe. This module helps you build trust gradually, learn to depend on others appropriately, and develop secure relationships.
  • You'll learn that trust doesn't mean blind faith—it means choosing to depend on others while maintaining your own strength.
  • Trust is a skill. You can learn it. This module shows you how.
Key Takeaways
  • Trust is earned gradually: you don't have to trust everyone immediately.
  • Trust doesn't mean losing yourself: you can depend on others and still be strong.
  • Small acts of trust build bigger trust: start small and build from there.
  • Trust is worth the risk: the benefits of connection outweigh the fear.

Understanding trust

Trust is the belief that others are reliable, that they'll be there when you need them, that they have your best interests at heart. For people with avoidant attachment, trust can feel dangerous.

You've learned that depending on others is unsafe. Maybe people have let you down. Maybe you've been disappointed. Maybe you've learned that the only person you can trust is yourself.

But trust is possible. You can learn to trust others while maintaining your own strength. You can depend on others without losing yourself.

Exercise 1

Trust assessment: How much do you trust others? Rate yourself 1–10. What makes trust difficult? What would help?

Building trust gradually

Trust doesn't have to be all-or-nothing. You can build it gradually. Start with small acts of trust: Share a small feeling. Ask for a small favor. Accept a small gesture of support.

Notice how it feels. Notice how others respond. When small acts of trust work out, you can try bigger ones. Trust builds on itself.

You don't have to trust everyone. Choose people who have shown themselves to be reliable. Start with those you feel safest with. Build from there.

Exercise 2

Trust practice: Choose one small act of trust this week. Share a feeling. Ask for help. Accept support. Notice how it feels.

Learning to depend on others

Depending on others doesn't mean losing your independence. It means recognizing that you don't have to do everything alone. It means accepting help, support, and connection.

Practice depending on others: Ask for help with something. Share a problem and ask for advice. Accept emotional support. Let someone be there for you.

Start small. Ask for a small favor. Accept a small gesture of support. Notice how it feels. Notice that you're still you, still strong, still independent—just connected.

Exercise 3

Trust and boundaries

Trust doesn't mean having no boundaries. You can trust others and still maintain boundaries. You can depend on others and still be independent.

Healthy trust involves: Choosing who to trust (not everyone deserves your trust), Trusting gradually (building trust over time), Maintaining boundaries (trusting doesn't mean losing yourself), and Trusting yourself (you can trust others and still trust yourself).

You can be both trusting and discerning. You can depend on others and still maintain your strength. You can be connected and independent.

The rewards of trust

Trust has rewards: Deeper relationships, Emotional support, Shared burdens, Greater connection, and A sense of belonging.

These rewards are worth the risk. When you can trust others, you can build relationships that are both supportive and healthy, connected and respectful.

Trust is a skill. It takes practice. But it's worth it. The connections you build will enrich your life.
