Module 6 • Secure Communication: Expressing Needs and Emotions

Overview
  • Avoidant attachment often involves difficulty expressing needs and emotions. This module teaches you to communicate clearly, express yourself authentically, and build connection through communication.
  • You'll learn that expressing needs doesn't make you weak—it makes you human. You'll learn to communicate in ways that create connection rather than distance.
  • Secure communication is a skill. You can learn it. This module shows you how.
Key Takeaways
  • Expressing needs is healthy: it's human to have needs and express them.
  • Communication creates connection: sharing yourself builds relationships.
  • Vulnerability is strength: expressing yourself authentically requires courage.
  • Clear communication prevents problems: expressing needs directly avoids misunderstandings.

The challenge of expressing needs

If you have avoidant attachment, you might struggle to express needs. You might believe you shouldn't have needs. You might think expressing them makes you weak or dependent.

But needs are human. Everyone has them. Expressing them is healthy. When you can express needs clearly, others can respond. When you can't, you stay isolated.

The goal isn't to become needy—it's to express needs appropriately. You can have needs and still be independent. You can express them and still be strong.

Exercise 1

Need identification: List 5 needs you have. Which are easy to express? Which are difficult? Why?

Learning to express needs

Expressing needs is a skill. You can learn it. Start by identifying your needs: 'I need [specific need].' 'It would help me if [specific request].' 'I'd appreciate [specific action].'

Practice expressing needs: Start small. Express one need. Make it specific. Make it clear. Notice how it feels. Notice how others respond.

Expressing needs doesn't make you weak—it makes you human. It's an act of trust and connection. When you express needs, you're saying: 'I trust you enough to share what I need.'

Expressing emotions clearly

Expressing emotions can be challenging when you have avoidant attachment. You might suppress them, dismiss them, or not notice them. But expressing emotions is key to connection.

Practice expressing emotions: Name the emotion ('I'm feeling [emotion]'), Share the context ('When [situation], I feel [emotion]'), Express the need if relevant ('I need [specific need]'), and Invite connection ('Can we talk about this?').

Start small. Express one emotion. Share one feeling. Notice how it feels. Notice how others respond. Build from there.

Active listening and understanding

Communication isn't just about expressing—it's about understanding. Active listening builds connection.

Active listening: Give full attention, Reflect back what you heard ('So what I'm hearing is...'), Ask clarifying questions ('Can you help me understand...?'), and Validate their experience ('That makes sense. I can see why you'd feel that way.').

When you listen actively, you understand others better. You see their perspective. You build trust. This creates connection even when you're not expressing yourself.

Communication and boundaries

You can communicate clearly and still maintain boundaries. Expressing needs doesn't mean losing yourself. Sharing emotions doesn't mean being overwhelmed.

Healthy communication involves: Expressing yourself clearly, Listening actively, Maintaining boundaries, Respecting others' boundaries, and Finding balance between sharing and protecting yourself.

You can be both open and protected. You can share yourself and still maintain your strength. You can communicate and still be independent.
