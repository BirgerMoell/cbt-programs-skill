Module 5 • Sustainable Sleep: Trusting the Process

Overview
  • The final module moves from strict intervention to sustainable maintenance. We discuss how to safely expand your sleep window as your efficiency improves.
  • We address 'Sleep Confidence'—the belief that you can sleep. We review the evidence of your progress.
  • Relapse prevention is key. We identify triggers (stress, travel, illness) that might disturb sleep and create a plan to handle them without panicking.
  • We explore the concept of 'Good Enough Sleep.' Letting go of the perfectionist ideal of 8 solid hours every night.
  • You will write a 'Sleep Credo'—a set of reminders to keep you grounded when you have a rough night.
  • We discuss how to handle jet lag and shift work using the principles you've learned.
  • Finally, we celebrate the shift in identity from 'Insomniac' to 'Person who sleeps adequately most of the time.'
Key Takeaways
  • One bad night is just one bad night; it is not a return to insomnia.
  • You can expand your time in bed slowly as long as sleep efficiency stays high.
  • Letting go of the need for 'perfect' sleep is the final key to good sleep.
  • You have the tools to manage any future disruptions.

Expanding the window

By now, you are likely sleeping more solidly, even if the duration is short. You are hungry for sleep, and you fall asleep faster. This is success.

Now we can gently loosen the reins. We will add time to your sleep window in 15-minute increments. We do this slowly, like checking the ice before stepping out. If you start spending time awake in bed again, we pull back. We are looking for the sweet spot.

Exercise 1

The Expansion Test: If your sleep efficiency was >90% last week, add 15 minutes to your bedtime (go to bed 15 mins earlier). Monitor for a week.

The bad night backup plan

You will have bad nights again. You will get sick, you will have a crisis, or you will just be human. The difference now is that you don't have to panic.

In the past, a bad night meant 'The insomnia is back!' Now, a bad night means 'I had a bad night.' You know what to do. You get up if you're not sleeping. You don't nap. You stick to your wake time. You trust your body to correct itself the next night.

You are no longer helpless. You are the pilot of your own sleep system.

Exercise 2

Relapse Plan: Write down: 'If I have 3 bad nights in a row, I will...' (e.g., return to strict window, stop caffeine, restart diary).

Good enough is perfect

The perfect sleeper does not exist. Everyone wakes up. Everyone has trouble sometimes. The goal was never perfect coma-like sleep. The goal was sleep that fuels your life.

If you wake up and feel okay to get through the day, you have slept enough. Trust your daytime energy, not just the number on the clock. You have befriended the night, and it will take care of you.

Exercise 3

Sleep Credo: Write 3 statements to read when anxious. (e.g., 'My body knows how to sleep,' 'I can survive being tired,' 'Rest is enough').
