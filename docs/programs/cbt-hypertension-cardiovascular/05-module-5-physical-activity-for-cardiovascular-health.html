Module 5 • Physical Activity for Cardiovascular Health

Overview
  • This module addresses the role of physical activity in blood pressure management and overall cardiovascular health.
  • We explore 'How Exercise Lowers Blood Pressure'—the physiological mechanisms by which movement benefits the heart.
  • You will learn about 'Aerobic Exercise' for heart health—activities that strengthen the cardiovascular system (walking, swimming, cycling, etc.).
  • The concept of 'Moderate-Intensity Activity' is explained. You don't need to run marathons—moderate activity is highly effective.
  • We address 'Safety Considerations'—understanding your limits, monitoring exertion, and knowing when to consult your healthcare provider.
  • You will create a 'Cardiovascular Activity Plan'—a realistic, sustainable approach to increasing physical activity.
  • Finally, we practice 'Activity Pacing'—building activity gradually to prevent injury and ensure sustainability.
Key Takeaways
  • Regular physical activity is one of the most effective non-pharmacological treatments for hypertension.
  • Moderate-intensity activity is sufficient—you don't need to push to extremes.
  • Consistency matters more than intensity.
  • Activity should be enjoyable and sustainable to be effective long-term.

Movement as medicine

Physical activity is powerful medicine for your heart. It strengthens your heart muscle, improves blood vessel function, and can lower blood pressure by 5-7 mmHg—comparable to some medications.

But you don't need to become an athlete. Even moderate activity—brisk walking, gardening, dancing—provides significant cardiovascular benefits. The key is consistency.

We're going to find activities you enjoy. Because if you enjoy it, you'll do it. And if you do it regularly, your heart will thank you.

The cardiovascular benefits

When you're active, your heart gets stronger. It pumps more efficiently. Your blood vessels become more flexible. Your body becomes better at using oxygen. All of this lowers blood pressure and reduces cardiovascular risk.

These benefits are cumulative. Every walk, every movement session, every moment of activity adds up. You're building cardiovascular fitness, one step at a time.

We're aiming for 150 minutes of moderate-intensity activity per week. That's 30 minutes, 5 days a week. Or broken into smaller chunks—10 minutes here, 20 minutes there. It's achievable.

Exercise 2

Starting safely

If you're new to exercise or have cardiovascular concerns, safety is paramount. Start slowly. Build gradually. Listen to your body. Consult your healthcare provider if needed.

We're going to begin with low-intensity activities and gradually increase. We're going to monitor how you feel. We're going to respect your limits while gently expanding them.

The goal isn't to push through pain or exhaustion. The goal is sustainable movement that supports your heart health long-term.
