Module 3 • Medication Adherence: Making It Stick

Overview
  • This module addresses one of the biggest challenges in hypertension management: taking medication consistently as prescribed.
  • We explore 'Barriers to Adherence'—common reasons people miss doses (forgetting, side effects, cost, beliefs about medication, etc.).
  • You will learn 'Adherence Strategies'—practical tools to remember medications: pill organizers, phone alarms, habit stacking, visual reminders.
  • The concept of 'Medication Beliefs' is introduced. Negative beliefs ('I don't want to be dependent') can undermine adherence. We'll challenge and reframe these.
  • We address 'Side Effect Management'—working with your healthcare provider to minimize or manage medication side effects.
  • You will create a 'Medication Routine'—integrating medication-taking into your daily life so it becomes automatic.
  • Finally, we practice 'Problem-Solving for Adherence'—addressing specific barriers as they arise.
Key Takeaways
  • Medication adherence is critical for blood pressure control.
  • Forgetting is normal; systems prevent forgetting.
  • Side effects can often be managed; don't suffer in silence.
  • Your beliefs about medication affect your adherence.

The adherence challenge

Taking medication consistently sounds simple. But life gets busy. You forget. You travel. You run out. You feel fine and think you don't need it. Adherence is harder than it seems.

But here's the reality: Medication only works if you take it. Missing doses means your blood pressure isn't controlled. Your heart is still under stress. The risks remain.

We're going to make adherence easier. We're going to build systems. We're going to address barriers. We're going to make medication-taking as automatic as brushing your teeth.

Why adherence matters

Blood pressure medications work by keeping your blood pressure in a healthy range. But they only work while they're in your system. Miss a dose, and your blood pressure can spike.

Consistent adherence means consistent protection. Your heart gets a break. Your arteries get relief. Your risk of complications decreases.

Think of medication as daily maintenance for your cardiovascular system. Like changing the oil in your car, it's preventive care that keeps everything running smoothly.

Building the habit

Medication adherence is a habit. And like any habit, it can be built. The key is consistency and systems.

We're going to use 'Habit Stacking'—attaching medication to something you already do (e.g., 'after brushing teeth, take medication'). We're going to use reminders (alarms, pill organizers, visual cues).

We're also going to address the psychological barriers. If you believe medication is 'bad' or that you 'shouldn't need it,' we'll explore those beliefs. Because beliefs drive behavior.
