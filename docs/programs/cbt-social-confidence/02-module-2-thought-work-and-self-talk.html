CBT Program

Module 2 • Thought Work and Self-Talk

Overview
  • Learn to catch cognitive distortions common in social anxiety (mind-reading, catastrophizing, all-or-nothing thinking).
  • Practice compassionate reappraisal that balances realism with hope.
  • Develop coping statements and grounding cues for anxious moments.
Key Takeaways
  • Thoughts are hypotheses, not facts—you can test them against evidence.
  • Balanced thinking means acknowledging both risks and resources.
  • Prepared coping statements give your brain a script when anxiety surges.

No narrative sections.

Exercises
  • Complete a Thought Record for one recent anxious interaction (Situation → Automatic Thought → Emotion → Evidence For → Evidence Against → New Balanced Thought).
  • Draft 3–5 coping statements that feel believable (e.g., "I can have a conversation imperfectly and still be valued.").
  • Practice a 2-minute grounding routine (breath, senses, posture) twice this week and note the effect.
Homework
  • Use Thought Records on two additional situations; highlight what changed your emotion intensity.
  • Carry one coping statement on your phone lock screen or sticky note and read it before a social interaction.
  • Track which grounding cue helps you return to the present fastest.
