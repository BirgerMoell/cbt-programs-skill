Module 5 • Boundaries and Self-Care: Maintaining Your Sense of Self

Overview
  • Anxious attachment often leads to losing yourself in relationships. This module teaches you to set boundaries, maintain your identity, and care for yourself while in relationships.
  • You'll learn that boundaries aren't walls—they're gates. They protect you while allowing connection. You'll learn to balance relationship needs with personal needs.

Key Takeaways
  • Boundaries protect you and your relationships: they're essential for secure attachment.
  • You can be close and independent: intimacy and autonomy can coexist.
  • Self-care isn't selfish: it's essential for healthy relationships.
  • Your identity matters: maintain it even in relationships.

Why boundaries matter

Anxious attachment often leads to boundary-less relationships. You merge with your partner. You lose yourself. You become dependent. You need constant connection to feel okay.

This feels like love, but it's not. It's enmeshment. It's losing yourself in another person. It's making your partner responsible for your happiness, your worth, your identity.

Boundaries are the solution. They're not walls that keep people out—they're gates that protect you while allowing connection. They define where you end and your partner begins.

With boundaries, you can be close and independent. You can love and be yourself. You can need your partner without needing them to complete you.

Exercise 1

Boundary audit: What boundaries do you have? Which are healthy? Which need work? Where do you lose yourself?

Setting healthy boundaries

Healthy boundaries include: Time boundaries (time for yourself, time for friends, time for hobbies), Emotional boundaries (your emotions are yours; your partner's are theirs), Physical boundaries (your body, your space), and Communication boundaries (when and how you communicate).

Setting boundaries: Identify your limits ('I need time alone each day'), Communicate them clearly ('I need an hour to myself in the evenings'), Enforce them consistently ('I'm going to take my time now'), and Respect your partner's boundaries too.

Boundaries aren't ultimatums—they're self-care. They're saying: 'I matter. My needs matter. I can be in a relationship and still be me.'

Exercise 2

Identity check: Who are you outside your relationship? What interests? What goals? What values? Write them down.

Maintaining your identity

In anxious attachment, identity often gets lost in relationships. You become 'we' and lose 'I.' This creates dependency and anxiety.

Maintain your identity by: Keeping your interests and hobbies, Maintaining friendships, Having your own goals, Spending time alone, and Remembering who you are outside the relationship.

Your identity is valuable. Don't lose it in relationships. When you know who you are, you're more secure. You don't need constant reassurance because you have yourself.

Exercise 3

Self-care plan: Create a self-care plan. What will you do daily? Weekly? How will you protect it?

Self-care in relationships

Self-care is essential for secure attachment. When you care for yourself, you're less dependent on your partner for validation and security.

Self-care includes: Physical care (sleep, nutrition, exercise), Emotional care (processing feelings, self-compassion), Mental care (learning, growth, stimulation), Social care (friendships, community), and Spiritual care (values, meaning, purpose).

Self-care isn't selfish—it's necessary. When you're cared for, you can care for others. When you're secure in yourself, you can be secure in relationships.

Make self-care non-negotiable. Schedule it. Protect it. It's as important as relationship time.

Balancing needs

Secure attachment involves balancing your needs with your partner's needs. It's not all about you, and it's not all about them—it's about both.

Practice: Identify your needs. Identify your partner's needs. Find ways to meet both. Sometimes you prioritize theirs. Sometimes they prioritize yours. It's a dance, not a battle.

When needs conflict, communicate. Negotiate. Find solutions that work for both. Secure relationships are collaborative, not competitive.
