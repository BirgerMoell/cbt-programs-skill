CBT Program

Module 1 • Understanding Your Autistic Brain & Setting Your Course

Overview
  • Welcome to the start of your journey. This foundational module is about building a solid base of self-knowledge. Before we can build strategies for navigating the world, we must first understand the unique and powerful architecture of the mind we're working with.
  • We'll shift the perspective from seeing autism as a set of deficits to understanding it as a different, valid way of processing and experiencing life. We'll set the stage for the practical, skill-building work to come by establishing a framework of acceptance and clear, meaningful goals.
  • Your brain runs on a distinct and powerful operating system. This autistic operating system has incredible strengths—an eye for detail, a capacity for deep focus, a strong sense of logic and justice, and an ability to see patterns others miss.
  • The goal of this program is not to try to rewrite your core operating system. Instead, the goal is to provide you with a user manual—a set of tools, translation guides, and strategies that allow your system to function effectively and thrive in a world that wasn't built with its specifications in mind.
Key Takeaways
  • You are not defective, you are different. Your brain runs on a distinct and powerful operating system.
  • CBT for autism uses the S-T-E-S-B Cycle: Situation, Thought, Emotion, Sensation, Behavior.
  • Understanding your sensory profile, information processing style, and special interests is foundational to building effective strategies.
  • Self-compassion is the antidote to a lifetime of external criticism. True progress is built on self-acceptance, not self-criticism.

Exercises
  • Create an initial profile snapshot: jot down one sensory sensitivity, one sensory joy, and one special interest that brings you into a state of focus.
  • Write your why: two sentences on why you chose this program right now and what you hope to gain.
  • Reframe one external expectation into an authentic, skill-based goal that focuses on reducing distress and increasing your capacity for joy.
Homework
  • My Autistic Profile: Create two columns—'Hypersensitive (Avoid)' and 'Hyposensitive (Seek)'. List at least 3–5 specific sensory experiences for each category across different senses.
  • Information Processing: Describe one situation where your literal interpretation led to a misunderstanding and what you learned. Describe one situation where your attention to detail was a major advantage.
  • Special Interests: List 2–3 of your most significant special interests. For each one, write a sentence about why it is important to you.
  • Refining Your Goals: Write 1–3 meaningful goals for yourself for this program, focusing on reducing distress or improving well-being, not just on 'fitting in.'
  • Self-Compassion Pledge: Write a short, personal self-compassion statement you can read to yourself when feeling overwhelmed or being self-critical.
