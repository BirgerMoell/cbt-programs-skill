Module 6 • Integrating Special Interests & Executive Function

Overview
  • This module focuses on two deeply interconnected aspects: the immense power of your special interests and the unique challenges of executive functioning. We'll explore how to leverage your passions not just for joy, but as a powerful tool for motivation and regulation.
  • Your special interest is a superpower: it's a source of joy and expertise, a powerful regulation tool that induces flow state, and a source of intrinsic motivation. Honoring and making time for your special interests is a fundamental act of self-care.
  • Executive function challenges for autistic individuals often look different from ADHD. The difficulty is often not distraction, but inertia and rigidity. Autistic inertia is the profound difficulty in starting (or stopping) a task, often caused by overwhelm from bottom-up processing or a need for a perfect plan.
  • Rigid sequencing is the feeling that tasks must be done in a specific order. The thought of starting with Step 3 when Step 1 isn't perfectly complete can feel deeply wrong, even if it would be more efficient.
  • The 'First, Then' Method (Premack Principle) links a non-preferred task to a preferred one, using your special interest as an immediate reward. The formula: First, you do what you need to do. Then, you get to do what you want to do.
  • The Chunking and Focusing Method combats overwhelm: Chunk the task into a complete list, then Focus on One Chunk Only by covering the list and working on just the first step. This honors your need for a complete plan while protecting you from overwhelm.
Key Takeaways
  • Special interests are superpowers: sources of joy, regulation, and motivation. They are fundamental to autistic well-being.
  • Autistic inertia and rigid sequencing are common executive function challenges that require specific strategies.
  • The 'First, Then' Method uses your special interest as immediate, guaranteed motivation.
  • Chunking and Focusing honors your need for a complete plan while protecting you from overwhelm.

Exercises
  • Interest-Based Motivation: Practice using the 'First, Then' method to complete one non-preferred task. Write down your statement with defined times for both activities.
  • Practice Chunking: Choose one large task that feels overwhelming. Create a master list of all steps, then use the Focus method—write only the first step on a sticky note and complete it.
  • Identify Your Inertia: Pay attention to a moment when you felt 'stuck' or unable to start a task. Analyze the cause: was it overwhelm, need for perfect plan, or rigid sequencing?
Homework
  • Interest-Based Motivation: Use the 'First, Then' method this week. Document the task, reward, terms, and reflect on whether it made starting easier.
  • Practice Chunking: Create a master list for an overwhelming project. Use the Focus method for at least three consecutive chunks. Document the experience.
  • Executive Function Log: Document a moment of autistic inertia. Describe the situation, analyze the cause, and brainstorm one strategy you could try next time.
