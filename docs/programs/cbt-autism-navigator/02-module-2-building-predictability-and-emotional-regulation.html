Module 2 • Building Predictability and Emotional Regulation

Overview
  • Having built a foundation of self-understanding, we now begin constructing the core framework for well-being: a stable and predictable environment, both externally and internally. The autistic brain thrives on order and expends a tremendous amount of energy processing a chaotic and sensorially overwhelming world.
  • Routines are not prisons; they are anchors in a chaotic sea. They reduce anxiety by making the immediate future predictable, prevent decision fatigue by automating small decisions, and provide comfort through repetition and predictability.
  • Autistic burnout is a state of profound physical, mental, and emotional exhaustion. The Spoon Theory helps us manage energy: imagine you wake up each day with a limited number of spoons, and every action costs a certain number. Being a good energy accountant means knowing the cost, budgeting your spoons, and finding ways to recharge.
  • A sensory diet is a carefully planned schedule of sensory activities designed to keep your nervous system regulated throughout the day. Instead of waiting to become overwhelmed, you can proactively provide your brain with the inputs it needs to stay calm and focused.
  • Emotional literacy can be challenging for many autistic individuals (alexithymia). Start with the body—notice raw physical sensations, then use tools like an emotion wheel to translate these sensations into emotional language.
Key Takeaways
  • Routines are anchors in a chaotic sea—they free up cognitive resources and provide comfort.
  • Energy accounting (Spoon Theory) is a critical self-management skill for preventing burnout.
  • A sensory diet proactively provides your brain with the inputs it needs to stay regulated.
  • Emotional literacy starts with the body: notice physical sensations first, then translate them into emotional language.

Exercises
  • Design a foundational routine: Choose either your morning or evening and design a simple, 3–5 step routine you can practice consistently for the next week.
  • Your Spoons: Identify one high-cost activity (3+ spoons) and one recharging activity that gives you spoons back or costs zero spoons.
  • Emotional detective: Practice noticing physical sensations in your body. Write down 3–5 raw physical data points (e.g., chest tight, face hot, hands clenching) without trying to name the emotion yet.
Homework
  • Energy Log: For at least three days this week, practice being an energy accountant. Create a log with columns: Activity | Predicted Spoon Cost (1–5) | Actual Spoon Cost (1–5) | Recharging Activity Used?
  • My Sensory Toolkit: Create categories for At Home, At Work/School, and In Public. For each category, list 2–3 specific items or actions you can use to regulate yourself. Choose one strategy and intentionally use it this week.
  • Foundational Routine Tracking: Note each day that you successfully complete your routine. How does it feel? What surprised you?
