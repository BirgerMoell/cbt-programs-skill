Module 2 • The Problem-Solving Engine

Overview
  • Diabetes problems often feel too big to solve (e.g., 'I want to lose weight but insulin makes it hard'). This module teaches a structured 4-step Problem-Solving technique to break vague worries into actionable plans.
  • We move away from emotional reasoning and toward logical strategy, helping you tackle issues regarding diet, insulin timing, and social navigation.
  • You will build a 'Solution Bank' to prevent decision fatigue.
Key Takeaways
  • A problem well-defined is a problem half-solved.
  • Brainstorming requires suspending judgment to find creative solutions.
  • Action plans must be specific, measurable, and realistic.

Step 1: Define the problem clearly

Vague problems like 'I'm messy with my supplies' are impossible to solve. We need specifics: 'I forget to restock my pump supplies because I don't have a visual reminder.'

In this module, you will list your top 3 diabetes frustrations. Be specific. Is it fear of lows at night? Is it awkwardness injecting in public? Is it burnout from tracking data?

Step 2 & 3: Brainstorming and Selecting

Generate as many solutions as possible without critiquing them. Even silly solutions count because they spark creativity. Then, pick the most viable one.

For example, if you forget evening insulin: Set an alarm? Put the pen on your pillow? Ask a partner to remind you? Link it to brushing your teeth?

Step 4: Action Planning

A solution is just a wish until it is planned. When will you start? What do you need? What could get in the way?

Exercises
  • Problem Selection: Choose one specific issue from your Distress Inventory (Module 1).
  • The Solution Generator: Write down 5 potential solutions for this problem, then rank them by feasibility.
Homework
  • The One-Week Test: Implement your chosen solution for 7 days. Do not judge it until the week is over.
