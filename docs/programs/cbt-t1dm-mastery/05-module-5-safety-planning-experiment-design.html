Module 5 • Safety Planning & Experiment Design

Overview
  • Exposure in T1DM is different from regular phobias because the risk is real. Therefore, safety planning is non-negotiable.
  • We integrate problem-solving here to mitigate *actual* risk while challenging *perceived* risk.
  • You will design a 'Behavioral Experiment' to test your fears safely.
Key Takeaways
  • Medical safety comes first; we expose to the *feeling* of fear, not dangerous medical states.
  • Preparation reduces the likelihood of catastrophic thinking.
  • Every exposure needs an 'exit strategy' to keep you feeling safe.

The safety net

We never expose to unsafe blood sugars. If you are actually low, treat it. We expose to the *situation* or the *sensation*.

We will set up safety rails: carrying fast-acting glucose, wearing medical ID, and setting CGM alerts. Once these are in place, the remaining fear is psychological.

Designing the experiment

Select the bottom rung of your ladder. Plan the When, Where, and How.

Example: 'I will walk to the mailbox without my oversized bag of supplies. I will have glucose tabs in my pocket, but I won't bring the whole bag.'

Exercises
  • Safety Checklist: Verify you have the necessary medical supplies for your planned exposure.
  • Prediction Log: Write down: 'I predict that if I do X, anxiety will be at level Y, and [Bad Thing] will happen.'
Homework
  • The Setup: Prepare your environment for the first exposure (e.g., pack the light bag, charge the phone).
