CBT Program

Module 4 • Building the Exposure Ladder

Overview
  • Exposure therapy is the gold standard for reducing fear. We will use a 'graded' approach, meaning we start small.
  • You will sort potential situations (e.g., going to the gym, sleeping alone) into a hierarchy from 'mildly uncomfortable' to 'terrifying.'
  • This preparation phase ensures you feel in control of the process.
Key Takeaways
  • We do not jump into the deep end; we climb a ladder.
  • Subjective Units of Distress (SUDs) help us measure fear objectively.
  • The goal is habituation: getting bored of the fear, rather than eliminating the risk.

Creating your hierarchy

Imagine a ladder. The bottom rung is something easy, like walking around the block without checking your CGM for 10 minutes. The top rung might be exercising alone.

We will look at 'cards' of common diabetes fears. You will sort them and select which ones apply to your life. This creates your personal roadmap.

Defining the feared outcome

What are you actually afraid of? 'If I go for a run, I will pass out and no one will find me.' We need to write this down so we can test if it's true.

Exercises
  • Card Sorting: Categorize 10 common situations into Green (Easy), Yellow (Challenging), and Red (Hard).
  • The Ladder Construction: Select 5 situations to form your exposure plan for the next few weeks.
Homework
  • Motivation Anchor: Write a letter to your future self about *why* you are doing this hard work (e.g., 'To hike with my family again').
