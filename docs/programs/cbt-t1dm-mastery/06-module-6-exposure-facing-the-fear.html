CBT Program

Module 6 • Exposure: Facing the Fear

Overview
  • This is the action phase. You will systematically engage with the situations on your ladder.
  • We focus on 'Expectancy Violation'—the powerful moment you realize the catastrophe you predicted did not happen.
  • You will learn to sit with high anxiety without performing a safety behavior (like checking your pump every 30 seconds).
Key Takeaways
  • Anxiety creates a bell curve; it rises, peaks, and eventually falls if you stay in the situation.
  • Learning happens when the 'bad thing' doesn't happen.
  • Repetition is essential for the brain to rewire its fear response.

Riding the wave

When you step into the feared situation, your anxiety will spike. This is expected. Do not retreat. Check your numbers to confirm safety, then wait.

Imagine anxiety as a wave. It cannot stay high forever. If you stay in the situation, the brain eventually realizes, 'Oh, we aren't dying,' and the anxiety drops.

Post-Exposure Review

After the activity, check your Prediction Log. Did you faint? Did you panic? Was it as bad as you thought?

Usually, the anticipation is worse than the event. This realization is the 'medicine' of CBT.

Exercises
  • Live Exposure: Complete your planned activity.
  • Debriefing: Record your starting anxiety, peak anxiety, and ending anxiety. Note what actually happened vs. what you feared.
Homework
  • Repetition: Repeat this specific exposure 3 times this week until it feels boring. Then move to the next rung on the ladder.
