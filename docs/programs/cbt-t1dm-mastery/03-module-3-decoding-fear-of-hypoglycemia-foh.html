Module 3 • Decoding Fear of Hypoglycemia (FoH)

Overview
  • Fear of Hypoglycemia (FoH) is a specific anxiety regarding low blood sugar. It is a survival instinct gone into overdrive.
  • FoH leads to 'safety behaviors'—like keeping blood sugar chronically high (running sweet) or avoiding exercise—that protect you today but hurt you tomorrow.
  • This module helps you distinguish between actual physiological hypoglycemia and the anxiety about hypoglycemia, which often feel identical.
Key Takeaways
  • Running blood sugar high to avoid lows is a common but dangerous coping mechanism.
  • Anxiety symptoms (sweating, racing heart) mimic low blood sugar symptoms.
  • Avoidance restricts your life and reinforces the fear.

The paradox of safety

It makes sense to fear lows. They are dangerous. But when fear dictates your life, you might stop exercising, stop driving, or eat unnecessarily. We call these 'safety behaviors.'

The problem is that avoidance prevents you from learning that you *can* handle a low. It traps you in a cycle where your world gets smaller and smaller.

The false alarm: Anxiety vs. Hypo

Here is the tricky part: Anxiety causes adrenaline release. Hypoglycemia causes adrenaline release. They feel the same (shaking, sweating, heart pounding).

Many people treat a panic attack with sugar because they think it's a low. We will learn to check the data (CGM/Fingerstick) before reacting.

Exercises
  • Safety Behavior Audit: List things you do to prevent lows that might be excessive (e.g., carrying 5 juice boxes for a 10-minute walk, keeping glucose above 12 mmol/L).
  • Symptom Differentiation: Create a checklist of your specific 'Low' symptoms vs. your 'Anxiety' symptoms.
Homework
  • The Pause Technique: This week, whenever you feel 'low,' commit to checking your number before eating (unless it is an obvious emergency).
