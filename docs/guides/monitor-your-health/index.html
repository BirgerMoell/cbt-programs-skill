Keeping Track of Your Health

Summary

Learn simple routines to observe body signals, record changes, and spot patterns that matter.

Self-carePreventionLifestyle

Build a daily check-in

Set a recurring reminder to note sleep, mood, energy, and pain levels using a scale from 1 to 5.

Scan for new or changing symptoms such as swelling, shortness of breath, rashes, or unusual bleeding.

  • Compare notes with your medication schedule so you can see how treatments affect how you feel.

Track key measures safely

Keep tools like a thermometer, blood pressure cuff, or glucose meter calibrated per manufacturer instructions.

Record measurements in one place—an app, notebook, or spreadsheet—and mark the time and context (e.g., fasting, after exercise).

  • Take devices to your next appointment so a nurse can confirm your technique and accuracy.

Notice patterns and triggers

Review your log weekly to spot trends, such as fatigue after poor sleep or headaches after specific foods.

Flag any readings outside your usual range with a star so they are easy to point out to your clinician.

  • Share summaries with trusted family members if they help you manage care or recognize warning signs.
