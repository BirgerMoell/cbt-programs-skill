Managing Alcohol Mindfully

Summary

Understand your drinking patterns, reduce risk, and get support when alcohol starts to impact your life.

Substance UsePreventionMental Health
alcoholharm reductioncravings

Check your current risk level

Track how many standard drinks you consume each day and compare with low-risk guidelines (e.g., no more than 14 per week for most adults).

Notice situations that lead to drinking more than planned, such as stress, social pressure, or boredom.

  • Consider using screening tools like AUDIT-C or a brief chat with your clinician to assess risk.

Set limits and alternatives

Decide on alcohol-free days each week and plan enjoyable activities—exercise, hobbies, or meeting friends for coffee instead of drinks.

Alternate alcoholic drinks with water or alcohol-free options and eat before or while drinking to slow absorption.

  • Share your plan with a trusted friend who can support your goals when you are out together.

Seek support early

Talk to your healthcare provider if you find it hard to cut back, experience cravings, or drink to manage emotions.

Explore counseling, support groups, or digital programs focused on substance use change strategies.

  • If you experience withdrawal symptoms—tremors, sweating, rapid heartbeat—seek medical help before stopping abruptly.
