Lifting Low Mood with CBT Skills

Summary

Build small, repeatable actions that improve mood and challenge unhelpful thinking patterns.

Spot the mood shifts

Track what was happening before, during, and after low mood episodes to uncover patterns.

Notice physical changes like slowed movement, heavy limbs, or fatigue as early signals.

  • Use a daily rating scale (0–10) in your journal to see trends over time.

Challenge the thought loop

Write the automatic thought, then list evidence for and against it to build a balanced alternative.

Replace "always/never" language with specific observations to stay grounded.

  • Most thoughts are interpretations, not facts—look for neutral explanations too.

Schedule energy-building activities

Combine one pleasure activity (reading, music, nature) with one mastery task (finishing a chore) each day.

Plan them on your calendar when energy is highest, even if motivation feels low.

  • Ask a friend to join or check in after the activity for accountability.
Progress Check
  • I attempted at least one practice prompt.
  • I can summarize what felt most useful.
  • I chose one small next step to try.

You do not have to be perfect to move on. The goal is honest effort and reflection.
